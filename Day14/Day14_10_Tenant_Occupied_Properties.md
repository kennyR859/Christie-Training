# Day 14-10: Tenant Occupied Properties

## Overview

Tenant-occupied properties require special attention in contracts. New Jersey strongly favors tenants, making it critical to identify all occupants, verify lease terms, and clearly define whether the buyer is accepting the property occupied or vacant.

---

## Minimum Required Information

### What You Must Know

> "At a bare minimum, the information you should have is what units are occupied and how much rent they're paying and if they're under a lease when the lease is terminating."

**Essential Information:**
| Question | Why It Matters |
|----------|----------------|
| Which units occupied? | Defines scope of tenancy |
| Rent amount? | Affects investment value |
| Lease term? | When can tenant leave? |
| Occupied or vacant at closing? | Buyer's expectations |

### Additional Important Details

- Security deposits (transfer to buyer)
- Lease terms and conditions
- Tenant contact information
- Payment history
- Any disputes or issues

---

## Why This Is Critical

### Late Discovery Problem

> "I've had a few transactions where we don't find out till after the fact that it's tenant occupied and then it's a mess because are they getting out? Are they not getting out? We're already under contract and agreed to close the way it is."

**Consequences of Late Discovery:**
- Contract may not address tenancy
- Closing complications
- Buyer surprises
- Potential legal issues

### Protecting All Parties

> "Not only because you want to do well for your buyers, but you also want to protect yourself."

**Protection Needed For:**
- Buyer: Know what they're getting
- Seller: Clear obligations defined
- Agent: Proper disclosure documented

---

## Verifying Lease Information

### Standard Attorney Review Process

> "My rider usually says within X amount of days we asked for copies of the leases and they're subject to the buyers review and approval."

**Process:**
1. Request lease copies within X days
2. Buyer reviews lease terms
3. Verify information matches seller's statements
4. Buyer can cancel if discrepancies

### Watch for Discrepancies

> "We don't want the seller saying 'well I'm getting $1,850 for this' and then we got a lease that says $1,200. You know, your client's screwed after that."

**Red Flags:**
- Rent amount doesn't match lease
- Lease terms different than represented
- Missing signatures
- Verbal "agreements" not documented

---

## Legal vs. Illegal Occupants

### The Critical Distinction

> "We have to make sure that these people have... that the apartments they are occupying are legal and that they actually have leases."

**Legal Occupant:**
- In legal apartment (CO for unit)
- Has valid lease agreement
- Proper tenant-landlord relationship

**Illegal Occupant:**
- No lease agreement
- In illegal unit (no CO)
- May be squatter or holdover

### Illegal Tenant Liability

> "There are significant damages involved with evicting an illegal tenant. We cannot have a situation where we let a buyer go in and assume responsibility for an illegal tenant."

**New Jersey Tenant Protections:**
- Moving costs as damages
- Six months of rent as relocation damages
- Court process required for eviction
- Significant legal fees

> "New Jersey is a state where everything favors the tenants."

---

## Accepting Property Occupied vs. Vacant

### Occupied at Closing

**Buyer Accepts:**
- Existing tenant stays
- Lease terms transfer
- Security deposit transfers
- Becomes new landlord

**Considerations:**
- Lease may limit rent increases
- Tenant may have rights
- Can't just ask them to leave

### Vacant at Closing

**Seller Must:**
- Ensure tenant leaves
- May need to serve notice
- Handle any eviction
- Bear all costs

**What Buyer Gets:**
- Empty property
- No tenant obligations
- Fresh start

---

## Notice to Quit Requirements

### When Needed

**Notice Required When:**
- Tenant not leaving voluntarily
- Lease has expired
- Going to owner-occupancy
- Sale requires vacancy

### Process Complexity

> "If I find out that there's a notice to quit that has to be served, there's going to be an issue. I have an attorney that I refer all landlord tenant stuff to."

**Why Specialize:**
- Landlord-tenant law is nuanced
- Language matters significantly
- Mistakes can be costly
- Process is time-sensitive

---

## Contract Provisions

### What to Include

**In Contract:**
- Number of units
- Occupied/vacant status
- Closing condition (occupied or vacant)
- Any known lease terms

**In Attorney Review Rider:**
- Lease review contingency
- Security deposit transfer
- Tenant notification requirements
- Vacancy guarantee if applicable

### Security Deposit Transfer

**Legal Requirement:**
- Security deposits transfer to buyer
- Seller provides documentation
- Buyer assumes landlord obligations
- Must follow NJ security deposit law

## Common Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Month-to-Month Tenant

**Situation:**
- Long-term tenant, no current lease
- Month-to-month tenancy
- Buyer wants property vacant

**Solution:**
- Seller must give proper notice
- Follow NJ notice requirements
- Allow time for vacancy
- May need to push closing

### Scenario 2: Long-Term Lease

**Situation:**
- Tenant has 2 years remaining on lease
- Buyer is investor, okay with tenant
- Lease terms are favorable

**Solution:**
- Lease review in attorney review
- Verify all terms
- Transfer security deposit
- Notify tenant of ownership change

### Scenario 3: Illegal Apartment

**Situation:**
- Basement apartment, no CO
- Occupant paying rent
- No formal lease

**Red Flags:**
- Illegal unit = major liability
- No lease = illegal occupant status
- Seller must resolve before closing
- Buyer should NOT accept responsibility

### Scenario 4: Rent Discrepancy

**Situation:**
- Seller says rent is $2,000
- Lease shows $1,600
- Tenant confirms $1,600

**Resolution:**
- Buyer must decide based on actual rent
- May renegotiate price
- Investment value affected
- Document everything

## Landlord-Tenant Law Considerations

### Tony's Approach

> "Landlord tenant... I really don't get into... the only way I'm involved with that is just in the closing capacity. I have an attorney that I refer like all landlord tenant stuff to very regularly."

**Why Specialization Matters:**
> "We all have our lanes and landlord tenant is very nuanced. And I don't like to start something that I'm not going to finish because you never know - like if there's one thing I learned in law school it's how much language matters. Everything can hinge on an AND or an OR."

### When to Refer Out

- Eviction proceedings needed
- Dispute with tenant
- Complex lease issues
- Rent control situations
- Illegal occupant removal

---

## Protecting Your Client

### For Buyer's Agents

1. **Get Full Information:** Don't proceed without complete picture
2. **Review Leases:** Ensure terms match representations
3. **Verify Legality:** Confirm apartments are legal
4. **Address in Contract:** Clear language on closing condition
5. **Document Everything:** Paper trail protects everyone

### For Listing Agents

1. **Gather Information Early:** Lease copies, rent amounts, terms
2. **Verify Apartment Legality:** CO for each unit
3. **Disclose Fully:** All tenant information in listing
4. **Coordinate with Seller:** Vacancy process if needed
5. **Prepare Documents:** For smooth transfer

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Know Before Contract:** Units, rents, lease terms, closing condition
2. **Verify Everything:** Don't trust verbal representations
3. **Legal Occupancy Critical:** Illegal tenants = major liability
4. **NJ Favors Tenants:** Eviction is expensive and difficult
5. **Specialize When Needed:** Refer landlord-tenant issues to experts
6. **Document Thoroughly:** Protect all parties
