## 1. Getting Attorneys Involved Early

### The Most Important Tip

> "My first and most important tip that I tell everybody all the time is get an attorney involved as early as possible in the process with your clients. That is going to be the biggest help for you in getting your offers through and also in getting through attorney review smoothly."

### Why Early Involvement Matters

**Problem Prevention:**
- Attorneys can help craft creative offers that get accepted while protecting clients
- Ensures clients understand what they're agreeing to BEFORE offers are submitted
- Prevents situations where clients say "I didn't agree to that" during attorney review

**Client Education:**
- First-time buyers have tons of questions
- Attorneys can explain nuances like tax reassessments on renovated homes
- More information upfront = fewer surprises after closing

### Practical Implementation

**For Buyers:**
- Introduce attorney before contract - even before having an offer accepted
- No payment or signing required until offer is accepted
- Attorney can answer questions during the offer process

**For Agents:**
- Develop relationships with attorneys you're comfortable with
- Call with general questions - it's not just for active transactions
- Treat it as a team effort with the same goal in mind

### Retainer Process

> "I have a retainer agreement. It acts like a guide letter for the closing. There's lots of information in there for the clients."

- Some attorneys have onboarding processes
- Better to complete before rushing to get a rider out
- Introduction call covers what to expect from attorney review to closing

## 2. Appraisal Contingency and Waivers

### The Common Misunderstanding

> "Probably nine times out of 10 when I have an offer like that come to me in attorney review, the client doesn't actually understand what they waived."

**Critical Fact:** The appraisal is ALWAYS done if there is a lender. What you're waiving is the ability to renegotiate based on the appraisal results.

### How Appraisal Waivers Work

**Example Scenario:**
- Purchase Price: $1,000,000
- Loan-to-Value Ratio: 80% (meaning $800,000 mortgage)
- Appraisal comes back at: $900,000
- Lender will only loan: 80% of $900,000 = $720,000
- **Gap the buyer must cover: $180,000 in cash**

### Types of Appraisal Waivers

1. **Full Waiver:** Buyer waives ANY deficiency - very risky
2. **Partial Waiver (Common):** Waive gap between listing price and offer price
   - Fair from seller's perspective: "I told you what I thought it was worth"
   - Buyer still needs to understand potential cash requirement
3. **Capped Waiver:** Buyer will cover up to $X of any gap

### Agent Responsibility

> "You can recommend it... but on the other hand you need to be cautious about what you're urging or pushing or recommending your clients to do because at the end of the day... they're going to turn around and point their fingers."

**Best Practice:**
- Suggest as a possibility, don't recommend
- Explain that many people are doing it
- Ensure client understands the financial implications
- Get attorney to help with correct language

### Appraisal Appeals

When an appraisal seems wrong:
- Review the appraisal report for errors (missed bathroom, wrong characteristics)
- Agents and clients often know the property better than the appraiser
- Appeal process is available but not always successful
- Sometimes need to switch lenders for a new appraisal

**Real Example:** Appraiser from South Jersey notorious for problems in a specific neighborhood - had to switch lenders mid-transaction.

## 3. Contract Basics - Names and Addresses

### Names

> "Make sure the buyers and sellers names are correct... sometimes the tax records are not always complete or accurate."

**Best Practices:**
- Ask clients for their ID or have them fill in exactly as on ID
- Simple mistakes cause delays (John Henry vs. John Henry Louis III)
- May require re-running searches and re-doing addendums

**Entities and Estates:**
- Get the name of the person authorized to sign
- If you don't have it, attorney will get it in attorney review

### Addresses

**Common Mistakes:**
- Missing unit numbers on condos
- Properties with two addresses (use tax record address, notify attorney)
- Properties in two different towns (requires OPRA in both, tax rates in both)

**Unusual Situations:**
- Some properties have addresses in two towns
- School district determined by where bedrooms are located
- Always queue the attorney to unusual address situations

## 4. Deposit vs. Down Payment

### The Critical Difference

> "A lot of agents will put the entire down payment on the deposit line. Why that's not good..."

**Deposit:**
- Paid upfront to seller side
- Held in escrow during transaction
- Refundable under contingencies (e.g., mortgage denial with good faith application)
- Security for seller if buyer breaches

**Down Payment:**
- Total amount buyer is putting toward purchase
- Deposit is PART of down payment

### Why Separation Matters

**Seller Credit Issue:**
- All loans have a cap on how much credit a buyer can receive from seller at closing
- If no funds at closing to offset credit, buyer loses the benefit

**Example:**
- House needs work, negotiated $20,000 credit
- Entire down payment already paid as deposit
- $5,000 of credit exceeds loan program limits
- Buyer loses that $5,000 at closing table

### FHA Considerations

- FHA requires only 3.5% down payment
- Sellers often want entire 3.5% as deposit (small amount of security)
- May need to include provision for price reduction if credit can't be applied

### Workarounds

- Reduce purchase price instead of credit
- Hope seller is cooperative
- Attorney adds protective language in rider

## 5. Loan Type Selection

### Why It Matters

> "Sellers put a lot of weight on that... if they see an FHA they're less likely to go with it."

**Seller Perceptions (somewhat outdated but persistent):**
- FHA seen as scary, complicated, slower
- FHA requires own appraisal inspection, sometimes repairs before closing
- Conventional seen as more solid, more qualified buyer

### Contract Breach Risk

If contract says "Conventional" and buyer switches to FHA:
- If FHA causes problems (repair requirements, appraisal issues)
- Buyer is technically in breach of contract
- Didn't get the loan type they said they were getting

### Attorney's Standard Rider Language

> "I have a provision in my rider that says that the buyer is allowed to change the loan program at any time during the course of the transaction provided it doesn't change the initial deposit or delay the commitment due date."

**Note:** Sometimes removed by other attorneys - seller may have relied on loan type to accept offer.

### Best Practice

- Be accurate about loan type in contract
- If wanting to switch, communicate with seller's side
- Explain buyer is qualified for both but FHA costs less
- Keep everyone on the same page to avoid breach issues

## 6. Items Included and Excluded

### The "As Per MLS" Problem

> "Nine times out of 10 attorneys say the MLS is not part of the contract. My client didn't write it. I don't want those representations to be included."

**Why It's Insufficient:**
- Attorneys don't consider MLS part of the contract
- Seller's attorney may refuse to be bound by MLS representations
- Need specific items spelled out in contract

### What to Specifically List

**High-Tech/Modern Items:**
- Nest systems
- Smart appliances (WiFi fridge)
- Gas/electric fireplace
- Additional appliances beyond standard

**Commonly Disputed Items:**
- Washer/dryer
- Light fixtures (if seller wants to take)
- Deck flooring (yes, someone tried to take it)

### Light Fixture Rule

If seller takes light fixtures:
- Must replace with "builder's grade" standard fixtures
- Cannot leave empty socket with light bulb

### Furniture and Personal Property

> "Attorneys don't like to get involved in that. You're paying a purchase price for the property itself, not for all the furniture."

**Issues with Including Furniture:**
- Transfer tax complications
- Better handled as side agreement
- Or create collective list outside contract

### Seller's Disclosure Cross-Reference

**Watch for Conflicts:**
- Seller's disclosure asks if items are "in working order"
- Seller checks "yes" for washer/dryer
- Contract says washer/dryer NOT included
- Creates confusion - clarify that "working" doesn't mean "included"

## 7. Time of Essence

### What It Means

> "If a date is time of essence, what that means is it's absolutely due today. There's no delays, no extensions. If you miss this due date, you're screwed."

### Standard Attorney Practice

**Paragraph 8 Usually Deleted:**
- Attorneys do not make contract due dates time of essence
- Provide notice and cure provisions instead
- Only closing date can typically be made time of essence

### Time of Essence Notice for Closing

**Process:**
1. Closing date in contract is NOT time of essence
2. If closing date is missed, can serve time of essence notice
3. Notice period: 14 days by law, often negotiated to 10 days
4. The date 10-14 days after notice becomes THE time of essence date
5. All parties must be ready, willing, and able to close by that date
6. Failure to close = contract cancellation + damages

### Contract Language Evolution

> "They're getting better... I've noticed different provisions have really started conforming to what we normally do now."

**Middle Paragraph Kept:** "All dates run from when attorney review is concluded" - this is legitimate and usually retained.

## 8. Certificate of Occupancy (CO)

### What It Is

- Application to town before closing
- Verifies no open permits or violations
- Town may do inspection and find issues

### The Liability Cap

**Standard Contract:** 1.5% of purchase price
**What Attorneys Actually Use:**
- Condo: $500
- Single Family or more: ~$1,000

### Why Caps Exist

**Seller's Perspective:**
- May need to make significant repairs
- Work done without permits may require removal (e.g., illegal basement kitchen)
- Bathroom sealed up, kitchen removed - common in Hudson County
- Seller may not want unlimited liability

**Buyer's Perspective:**
- May feel it's seller's problem
- But buyer gets use and benefit of any improvements
- Negotiation may share costs above the cap

### Best Practice for Buyer's Agent

- May leave blank from buyer's perspective
- Argue seller must give clean CO
- But understand seller's position in negotiations

## 9. Municipal Assessments

### The Rule

> "If the work is done before closing, even if it's not fully assessed yet, the sellers are responsible."

### What Are Municipal Assessments?

- Town-wide improvements charged to homeowners
- Examples: sidewalk replacement, water line replacement, lead pipe removal
- May be spread over multiple years in tax payments

### Handling Unknown Assessments

**When Work Is Not Yet Done:**
- Provide buyers with notice received during attorney review
- Buyer takes responsibility for future work
- Universal assessments (affecting all homes) are buyer's problem after closing

**When Work Is Done But Not Fully Assessed:**
- Establish escrow at closing
- Or agree to reasonable credit amount
- Sellers responsible even if assessment comes later

### Multi-Family Warning

> "Notice was sent out on a two family and instead of sending it... they sent it to the tenant."

- Town may send notices to tenants instead of owner
- Check for assessments when listing multi-family properties

### Fraud/Concealment Exception

- Representations generally don't survive closing
- Exception: fraud or willful concealment
- If seller received notice and didn't disclose, buyer can pursue after closing

## 10. Tenant Occupied Properties

### Minimum Required Information

At bare minimum, you need:
1. Which units are occupied
2. How much rent they're paying
3. When leases terminate (if under lease)
4. Whether buyer accepts property occupied or vacant

### Why This Is Critical

> "I've had a few transactions where we don't find out till after the fact that it's tenant occupied and then it's a mess."

**Problems with Late Discovery:**
- Are they getting out or not?
- Already under contract to close as-is
- May have illegal occupants without leases

### Illegal Tenant Liability

> "There are significant damages involved with evicting an illegal tenant."

**New Jersey Tenant Protections:**
- Moving costs as damages
- Six months of rent as relocation damages
- Everything favors tenants in NJ

### Standard Attorney Review Process

- Rider requests copies of leases within X days
- Subject to buyer review and approval
- Verify rent amounts match what seller represented
- If discrepancy found, buyer can cancel

### Lease Verification

**Watch For:**
- Seller says rent is $1,850
- Lease shows $1,200
- Buyer would be stuck with lower rent

## 11. Lead Safe Certifications

### 2024 Requirement

> "Sellers were required as of 2024 to have gotten lead safe certifications done, lead based paint inspections on certain properties."

**Timeline:**
- Due by 2024 deadline OR
- Within tenant turnover, whichever is sooner

### For Listing Agents

**Get Ahead of It:**
- When listing tenant-occupied property, ask seller about lead safe certifications
- If not done, go to town to find out requirements
- Don't let it be a last-minute closing delay

### Municipal Variations

- Some municipalities handle entire process (fill out form, they send inspector)
- Others require private testing, then submit to municipality
- Platform has list of recommended testers

## 12. Inspection Contingency

### Standard Attorney Practice

> "Most of the time we completely rewrite this entire contingency in attorney review."

**What Gets Changed:**
- Timeframes
- What's covered
- Who's responsible for what

### "Structural, Environmental, Mechanical, and Safety Only"

> "That's a full inspection... but I love it because you get away with looking like you're limiting something."

**Reality:** This covers essentially everything important - clever negotiating language.

### Standard Exclusions (Seller Side)

1. **Systems Beyond Useful Life:**
   - If working but old (e.g., 12-year-old water heater with 8-year expected life)
   - Seller not required to replace working equipment
   - Only actual defects must be addressed

2. **Upgrades:**
   - Be careful - some "upgrades" are required for code compliance
   - GFCI outlets example: technically an upgrade but safety/code requirement
   - Carve out: "unless it's a safety defect"

3. **Maintenance Recommendations:**
   - Items home inspector recommends but aren't defects
   - Example: "should service HVAC annually"

### Material Defects Definition

**By Statute:**
- Substantially affects safety, value, or habitability
- Does NOT include cosmetic or stylistic elements

### Additional Testing Requirements

**Seller Attorney Position:**
- If suspected asbestos, require lab confirmation before agreeing to anything
- Don't just rely on what home inspector "believes it may be"

**Special Cases Requiring More Time:**
- Septic systems
- Oil tanks (always removed, even if abandoned)
- If discharge found, need soil samples and remediation

### How Tony Works Inspections

1. Agents review report with clients first
2. Bring attorney a list of requests
3. Attorney reviews report for anything obvious that was missed
4. Attorney may suggest items commonly fixed
5. Also tells clients if requests are unreasonable

**Example of Unreasonable:** Asking seller to fix gutter extensions that cost $5 at Home Depot.

### Window Safety Note

> "Even with windows, people think it's not a big deal... that's a safety hazard if you can't open a window, if it slams shut."

## 13. Commission and Broker Fees

### Fill Out Completely

> "A lot of the times I don't see the seller side commission filled out... everybody always misses the MLS fee."

**What to Include:**
- Full commission amount
- MLS fee (often missed, people have lost this fee)
- Both buyer and seller side if applicable

### Attorney Review Rider Additions

**Tony's Standard Provisions:**
1. Repeats all commission information
2. Indemnification from other party
3. Protection if 3rd party claims commission
4. Protection if participating broker claims more than contract states

### Buyer Agency Agreement Conflicts

**Example Scenario:**
- Buyer signed agency agreement for 3%
- Seller only paying 2%
- Don't want buyer pursuing buyer's agent
- Don't want buyer's agent pursuing seller for extra 1%

### Documentation Trail

> "The more you have to support you... the better your case is."

**Keep Records Of:**
- Buyer's agency agreement with date and percentage
- Contract commission section
- Attorney review rider commission language
- Any modifications or agreements

## 14. Cash Buyers

### Two Key Concerns

> "There's two things where the homeless buyer could get into trouble."

1. **Insurance:**
   - No bank requiring insurance
   - Must ensure cash buyer gets insurance

2. **Appraisal:**
   - No lender requiring appraisal
   - Should still recommend getting one

### Why Cash Buyers Should Get Appraisals

> "This is why an appraisal is important: so if you want to get one, you're not going to be able to negotiate the purchase price, but you should at least know what you're buying is worth."

**Risk Without Appraisal:**
- No documented value at purchase time
- If market crashes, immediately underwater
- Appraisal provides record of value at purchase

### Proof of Funds

> "If somebody's coming to you saying I have $2 million to buy a house and I'm supposed to rely on your word and find out 45 days later that you don't..."

**Best Practice:**
- Request proof of funds as part of contract package
- Not necessarily agent's job to verify, but should ask for them
- Protects everyone in the transaction

## 15. OPRA Requests and Agent Liability

### The Responsibility Issue

> "If you're sending out an OPRA, you're now responsible for the information you received."

**The Risk:**
- Agent sends OPRA, receives information
- Agent doesn't study it thoroughly
- Something goes wrong based on that information
- Agent is now responsible for not communicating what they received

### Best Practice

**Instead of Agent Sending OPRA:**
1. Advise client they should do OPRA themselves
2. Direct client to municipality
3. Provide the schedule to the contract listing what to request
4. Let attorney handle OPRA

> "The more information you have, the more you're responsible for."

### OPRA Schedule

The contract includes a schedule of what buyers should request in OPRA:
- Building permits and their status
- Violations
- Assessments
- Other municipal records

## 16. Complete Document Package

### What Attorneys Need

> "Give me all the addenda. A lot of the times agents will just send over the 15 pages of the contract and the wire fraud."

**Complete Package Should Include:**
- Full contract (15 pages)
- Wire fraud disclosure
- MLS listing
- Seller's disclosure
- All addenda
- HOA addendum (if condo)
- HOA fee information
- Management company contact
- Any assessments information

### Why MLS Listing Matters

> "I always ask my agents for the MLS listing. Not everybody asks for it but it's important."

**Information Found There:**
- HOA fees
- Assessments
- Management company contact
- Property characteristics

### Tax Report

- Pull tax report as part of package
- Verify property information
- Cross-reference with contract

### The Copy Machine Method

> "One of the things I did to make life easier... went into the copy machine, copied it and emailed it to myself."

**Benefits:**
- Complete package always available
- Can send to attorney, listing agent, sellers, anyone
- Nothing gets lost

### Seller's Disclosure Importance

- Critical document for identifying issues
- May reveal things to discuss in attorney review
- Example: "Airport safety zone" checked by accident

## 17. Timeline Management

### Attorney Timeline Letters

> "I send timelines out at the conclusion of attorney review."

**What's Included:**
- Important dates
- Where to send deposit check
- Wire instructions
- Report due dates
- Commitment due date

### Key Dates to Track

1. **10% deposit due date**
2. **Inspection report due date**
3. **Mortgage commitment due date**
4. **Closing date**

### The Deposit Follow-Up Story

> "The agent reminded the buyer to drop the 10% off and she said... 'Oh yeah, my husband's stopping by and off today.' It's in his pocket till closing."

**Lessons:**
- Always follow up that deposit was actually received
- Two follow-throughs needed: remind client AND confirm with attorney
- Case law says attorneys have obligation to ensure deposit is paid

### Group Effort

> "Everybody's got to be on board and make sure everybody checks each other."

**Best Practice:**
- Put reminders in your phone
- Verify each step is completed
- Don't assume - confirm

## 18. Listing Agent Best Practices

### Get Ahead of Issues

> "If you're going to focus primarily on listings, it's great to get ahead of things on your side."

**Immediate Actions When Listing:**
1. File CO application immediately
2. Call building department - find out what's outstanding
3. Check for lead safe certifications
4. Determine if there's a well (water testing takes 2+ weeks)
5. Check for abandoned oil tanks

### Well Water Testing

> "Sellers are required to get the water tested before closing. Those water tests take a long time."

**Timeline Issues:**
- Tests take at least 2 weeks
- If remediation needed, add more time
- If trying to close cash deal in 3 weeks, already behind

### Oil Tank Proactive Approach

> "If there's an abandoned tank... buyers are not going to accept an abandoned tank anymore."

**Recommendation:**
- Check town records for tanks
- Have conversation with seller
- Schedule removal proactively (~$1,500)
- Be ahead of the game

### Why This Matters

> "Those are things that are good to address up front from the listing side."

- Prevents last-minute delays
- Makes property more attractive to buyers
- Demonstrates professionalism
- Protects seller from surprises
