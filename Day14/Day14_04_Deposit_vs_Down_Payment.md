# Day 14-04: Deposit vs. Down Payment

## Overview

Understanding the difference between deposit and down payment is crucial for contract writing and client protection. Many agents incorrectly treat these as interchangeable, which can cause significant problems at closing.

---

## The Critical Difference

### Deposit Definition

> "The deposit is what you're paying up front to the seller side. The deposit gets held in escrow during the transaction."

**Characteristics:**
- Paid at contract signing
- Held in escrow (usually attorney's trust account)
- Security for the seller
- Part of total down payment

### Down Payment Definition

**The Total Investment:**
- Full amount buyer is putting toward purchase
- Deposit is a PORTION of down payment
- Balance paid at closing
- Depends on loan program

### Visual Breakdown

**Example: $500,000 Purchase with 20% Down**

| Component | Amount | When Paid |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Total Down Payment | $100,000 | Split |
| Deposit (Part 1) | $50,000 | At contract |
| Balance (Part 2) | $50,000 | At closing |
| Mortgage | $400,000 | At closing |

---

## Why Separation Matters

### The Common Mistake

> "A lot of agents will put the entire down payment on the deposit line. Why that's not good..."

**What Happens:**
- All funds go to escrow upfront
- No money comes to closing
- Can't offset seller credits
- Buyer may lose negotiated benefits

### Seller Credit Limitations

**The Problem:**
> "With all loans actually there's a cap on how much credit a buyer is able to receive from a seller at closing. If there are no funds at closing to offset the credit, the buyer can lose the benefit of that credit."

**Loan Program Credit Limits:**
| Loan Type | Max Seller Credit |
|-----------|-------------------|
| FHA | 6% of purchase price |
| VA | 4% of purchase price |
| Conventional (under 10% down) | 3% |
| Conventional (10-25% down) | 6% |
| Conventional (25%+ down) | 9% |

### Real Example

**Scenario:**
- House needs significant work
- Negotiated $20,000 credit for repairs
- Entire down payment paid as deposit
- No money coming to closing

**Problem:**
- $5,000 of credit exceeds loan limits
- No funds to apply credit against
- Buyer loses that $5,000 benefit

> "We're at the closing table and the buyers might lose that 5K because they didn't have any money to bring to closing to offset the credit."

---

## Deposit as Security

### Seller's Perspective

> "If the buyers breach the contract or there's a dispute about something that happens, the deposit funds are all that the seller has to hold on to."

**What Deposit Provides:**
- Security during transaction
- Pool of funds if buyer defaults
- Incentive for buyer to perform

### Not Liquidated Damages

> "If there's a dispute and the contract's canceled, the seller doesn't just retain it. That's called liquidated damages. We don't do that in New Jersey. New York does."

**Key Point:**
- Deposit doesn't automatically go to seller if deal fails
- Must be resolved through dispute process
- Both parties have claims until resolved

### Refundability

**Deposit IS Refundable When:**
- Buyer makes good faith mortgage application
- Lender denies commitment
- Buyer provides denial letter
- Contingencies not met (inspection, etc.)

**Deposit at Risk When:**
- Buyer breaches contract
- Buyer fails to perform
- Contingencies were waived or met

---

## FHA Special Considerations

### Low Down Payment Programs

**FHA Requirements:**
- Only 3.5% down payment required
- On $500,000 house = $17,500 down payment

**Seller's Concern:**
> "A lot of the times the sellers will want the whole 3.5% as deposit because it's not a lot of money."

**The Problem:**
- Entire down payment is deposit
- No funds at closing
- Can't receive full seller credits

### Workarounds

**Option 1: Price Reduction Agreement**
> "We have to put in that they'll agree to reduce the purchase price if they can't get the correct credit."

**Option 2: Structure Credit Carefully**
- Keep credit within limits
- Factor into purchase price negotiation

**Option 3: Accept Risk**
- Rare but happens
- Buyer accepts potential credit loss

---

## Practical Guidance

### How to Split Deposit/Down Payment

**General Rule of Thumb:**
- Deposit: 5-10% of purchase price
- Leave remainder for closing

**Example: $500,000 Purchase, 20% Down**
| Line Item | Amount |
|-----------|--------|
| Deposit Line | $25,000-$50,000 |
| Down Payment Line | $100,000 |
| Brings to Closing | $50,000-$75,000 |

### When Larger Deposit Makes Sense

**Situations:**
- Competitive market (shows commitment)
- Seller requests it
- No anticipated credits
- Buyer has ample reserves

### When Smaller Deposit Makes Sense

**Situations:**
- Credits being negotiated
- FHA/VA with limited funds
- Property needs work
- Closing cost assistance expected

---

## Contract Completion

### What Goes Where

**Deposit Line (Paragraph 5):**
- Amount paid at contract
- Who holds it (attorney/broker)
- When due

**Down Payment Line:**
- Total down payment amount
- May include deposit as part

### Clear Documentation

**In Contract:**
- Specify deposit amount clearly
- Note how balance will be paid
- Reference down payment total

**In Attorney Review Rider:**
- Clarify any credit issues
- Address potential shortfalls
- Include price reduction language if needed

---

## Common Questions

### "Why can't escrow money be used for credits?"

> "Because it's technically considered paid to the seller already. Even though the seller was not paid... for the loan purposes it would be viewed as the seller giving the money back."

### "Does deposit count toward down payment?"

**Yes.** Deposit is part of down payment.
- $100,000 down payment
- $50,000 deposit paid
- $50,000 due at closing

### "What if buyer only has 3.5%?"

Work with attorney to:
- Structure credit within limits
- Consider price reduction instead
- Add protective language to rider

---

## Risk Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Credit Can't Be Applied

**Situation:**
- Entire down payment as deposit
- $15,000 credit negotiated
- Loan only allows $10,000 credit

**Result:**
- Buyer loses $5,000 in credits
- Already paid all down payment
- No funds to apply credit against

### Scenario 2: Proper Structure

**Situation:**
- Deposit: $30,000
- Brings to closing: $20,000
- Credit: $15,000

**Result:**
- $15,000 credit applied to closing funds
- Buyer only brings $5,000 to closing
- Full credit benefit received

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Deposit ≠ Down Payment:** Deposit is PART of down payment
2. **Leave Room:** Don't put entire down payment as deposit
3. **Credits Need Offset:** Funds at closing required to receive credits
4. **FHA Complicates:** Low down payment means limited flexibility
5. **Attorney Can Help:** Include protective language in rider
