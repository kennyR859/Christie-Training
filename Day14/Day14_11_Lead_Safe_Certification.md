# Day 14-11: Lead Safe Certification Requirements

## Overview

As of 2024, New Jersey requires lead safe certifications for tenant-occupied properties. This requirement can cause closing delays if not addressed proactively, especially when listing rental properties.

---

## The 2024 Requirement

### What Changed

> "Sellers were required as of 2024 to have gotten lead safe certifications done, lead based paint inspections on certain properties either by that due date or within the tenant turnover, whichever was sooner."

**Key Deadlines:**
- 2024 implementation date
- OR upon tenant turnover
- Whichever comes first

### Which Properties Affected

**Requires Lead Safe Certification:**
- Rental properties (1-4 units)
- Built before 1978
- Tenant-occupied

**May Be Exempt:**
- Owner-occupied single family
- New construction (post-1978)
- Previously certified

---

## What Lead Safe Certification Involves

### The Inspection Process

**Lead-Based Paint Inspection:**
1. Certified inspector examines property
2. Tests paint surfaces for lead
3. Identifies any lead hazards
4. Issues report of findings

### Certification Options

**Lead-Free Certificate:**
- Property has no lead paint
- Or lead has been properly removed/encapsulated
- Most desirable outcome

**Lead-Safe Certificate:**
- Lead may be present
- Currently in safe condition
- Interim controls in place
- Must be re-inspected periodically

---

## Impact on Real Estate Transactions

### For Sellers

> "When we're going to sell properties that are tenant occupied, it's important that the seller provides the lead safe certifications."

**Seller Obligations:**
- Provide certification to buyer
- Obtain if not already done
- Factor into closing timeline

### For Closings

**Without Certification:**
- Closing may be delayed
- Buyer's attorney may object
- Title company may require
- Lender may require for investment loans

---

## Getting Ahead of the Requirement

### For Listing Agents

> "If you're going to list a property that has tenants, ask the seller - did you get the lead safe certifications? If they didn't, go to the town and find out what's required and get it."

**Proactive Steps:**
1. Ask seller about certification status
2. Request copy of existing certification
3. If none exists, start process immediately
4. Factor timing into listing strategy

### Why Early Action Matters

> "There's no reason why it should be a last minute delay at the closing table. Just get ahead of it."

**Timeline Considerations:**
- Inspection scheduling
- Lab results if needed
- Re-inspection if issues found
- Remediation if required

---

## Municipal Variations

### How Different Towns Handle It

> "Some towns won't help you at all by the way... some of them do - they're like fill this out, we'll send somebody out, here's your certificate, great. Some of them say you have to go handle it privately and give it to us and then we'll give you the certificate."

**Town-by-Town Differences:**

| Town Type | Process |
|-----------|---------|
| Full Service | Town handles everything |
| Partial Service | Town issues cert, you arrange inspection |
| No Service | Private inspection, submit to town |

### Finding Resources

> "There is a list on... Platform of people to call or recommend that will go in and do the test."

**Resources:**
- Platform's recommended inspectors
- Municipal lists
- State-certified inspectors
- Lead abatement contractors

---

## The Inspection Process

### Who Can Perform Inspections

**Must Be Certified:**
- State-licensed lead inspector
- Certified risk assessor
- Approved testing methods

### What Gets Inspected

**Typical Inspection Points:**
- Window sills and frames
- Door frames
- Baseboards
- Exterior painted surfaces
- Any deteriorating paint
- High-friction surfaces

### Common Findings

**Issues That May Be Found:**
- Peeling or chipping paint
- Deteriorated painted surfaces
- High lead levels in paint
- Previous improper remediation

---

## When Remediation Is Required

### Types of Remediation

**Interim Controls:**
- Painting over lead paint
- Repairing deteriorated surfaces
- Cleaning and maintaining

**Abatement:**
- Permanent removal of lead
- Encapsulation
- Complete replacement
- More expensive, more permanent

### Cost Considerations

**Typical Costs:**
- Inspection: $200-500
- Interim controls: Varies widely
- Full abatement: $5,000-15,000+

---

## Contract Considerations

### What to Include

**In Contract:**
- Request certification status
- Contingency for certification
- Responsibility for obtaining

**In Attorney Review:**
- Deadline for providing certification
- Who pays for new inspection if needed
- Handling of remediation costs

### Allocation of Responsibility

**Typical Allocation:**
- Seller provides existing certification
- If none exists, seller obtains
- Remediation responsibility negotiable
- May affect purchase price

## Timeline for Lead Safe Process

### Best Case Scenario

| Step | Timeline |
|------|----------|
| Schedule inspection | 1-2 weeks |
| Inspection performed | Same day |
| Results received | 1-3 days |
| Certificate issued | 1-2 weeks |
| **Total** | **2-4 weeks** |

### If Remediation Needed

| Step | Timeline |
|------|----------|
| Inspection | 2 weeks |
| Remediation | 2-4 weeks |
| Re-inspection | 1-2 weeks |
| Certificate | 1-2 weeks |
| **Total** | **6-10 weeks** |

## Best Practices

### For Listing Agents

1. **Ask at Listing Appointment:**
   - "Do you have lead safe certification?"
   - "When was property built?"
   - "When did you last have inspection?"

2. **If No Certification:**
   - Explain requirement
   - Recommend immediate action
   - Provide inspector contacts
   - Factor into timeline

3. **Include in Marketing:**
   - Note certification status
   - Reduces buyer concerns
   - Speeds up transaction

### For Buyer's Agents

1. **Verify Certification:**
   - Request copy in offer
   - Review in attorney review
   - Confirm validity

2. **If Missing:**
   - Make contingent on receipt
   - Negotiate timeline
   - Consider inspection credit

## Compliance Documentation

### What to Keep on File

**For Transaction:**
- Lead safe certificate
- Inspection report
- Remediation records (if any)
- Municipal acknowledgment

### Disclosure Requirements

**Must Disclose:**
- Known lead paint
- Inspection results
- Remediation history
- EPA pamphlet provided

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **2024 Requirement:** All tenant-occupied pre-1978 properties need certification
2. **Get Ahead:** Don't wait until under contract
3. **Municipal Variations:** Process differs by town
4. **Timeline Impact:** Can add weeks to closing
5. **Remediation Possible:** May need repairs before certification
6. **Document Everything:** Keep certification on file
