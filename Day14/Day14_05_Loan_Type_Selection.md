# Day 14-05: Loan Type Selection and Changes

## Overview

The loan type specified in the contract matters more than many agents realize. Sellers make decisions based on loan type, and changing loan programs mid-transaction can create breach of contract issues.

---

## Why Loan Type Matters to Sellers

### Seller Perception

> "Sellers put a lot of weight on that when they accept the offers... if they see an FHA they're less likely to go with it."

**Common Seller Beliefs:**
| Loan Type | Perception |
|-----------|------------|
| Conventional | More qualified buyer, faster closing |
| FHA | Complicated, slower, repair requirements |
| VA | Similar concerns to FHA |
| Cash | Best - fastest, fewest complications |

### Historical Context

> "FHA loans were always seen as these scary, terrible long things that... do their own appraisal inspections, they require repairs before closing, they take so much longer."

**The Reality Today:**
> "They're not that bad anymore. They still do their own appraisal inspection, sometimes they do require a repair before closing, but... it's not that bad as it used to be."

### But Perception Persists

Even though FHA has improved:
- Sellers still prefer conventional
- Multiple offers = FHA gets passed over
- Conventional seen as "safer" acceptance

---

## Contract Breach Risk

### The Scenario

**What Happens:**
1. Contract says "Conventional"
2. Seller accepts based on that
3. Buyer switches to FHA mid-transaction
4. FHA causes problems (repairs, appraisal)
5. Buyer wants to cancel

**Legal Problem:**
> "Technically they're in breach of contract. They went under contract saying that they were applying for a conventional loan. It's not this other problem - you didn't get an FHA loan, you didn't tell me you were applying for an FHA loan."

### Why This Matters

**Seller's Position:**
- Accepted offer based on loan type
- Relied on conventional approval
- Now dealing with FHA complications
- Buyer can't use FHA issues to cancel

---

## Attorney's Standard Language

### Flexibility Provision

> "I have a provision in my rider that says that the buyer is allowed to change the loan program at any time during the course of the transaction provided it doesn't change the initial deposit or delay the commitment due date."

**Key Conditions:**
1. Doesn't affect deposit amount
2. Doesn't delay commitment date
3. Must be disclosed

### When It Gets Removed

> "Sometimes it gets taken out by the other attorney and I understand why because you're going into contract making an offer based on a specific loan program."

**Seller's Attorney Reasoning:**
- Seller relied on loan type
- Part of negotiated deal
- Should be binding

---

## Best Practices

### Be Accurate Upfront

**Select Correct Loan Type:**
- Know buyer's actual financing plan
- Don't guess or assume
- Verify with lender

**If Unsure:**
- Get clarification from buyer
- Confirm with mortgage professional
- Select what buyer will actually apply for

### If Wanting to Switch

**Communication Process:**
1. Disclose to seller's side early
2. Explain buyer qualifies for both
3. Demonstrate no added risk
4. Get agreement before switching

**Persuasive Arguments:**
> "Why do I have to pay more money out of pocket and get a higher rate just to appease the seller? I qualify for this loan, I want to go for this loan."

## FHA vs. Conventional Comparison

### Conventional Loans

**Advantages:**
- Faster perceived closing
- No FHA appraisal requirements
- More flexible property condition
- Higher seller confidence

**Requirements:**
- Higher credit score typically
- Larger down payment possible
- PMI if under 20% down

### FHA Loans

**Advantages:**
- Lower down payment (3.5%)
- More flexible credit requirements
- Lower rates sometimes
- Less cash needed upfront

**Perceived Disadvantages:**
- FHA appraisal requirements
- Potential repair requirements
- MIP (Mortgage Insurance Premium)
- Seller concerns

### FHA Appraisal Requirements

**What FHA Looks For:**
- Health and safety issues
- Structural integrity
- Required systems functioning
- Minimum property standards

**May Require Repairs:**
- Peeling paint (lead concern)
- Broken handrails
- Non-functional systems
- Safety hazards

## Switching Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Conventional to FHA (Most Common)

**Why It Happens:**
- Buyer realizes FHA saves money
- Conventional approval had conditions
- Down payment funds short

**Risk:**
- Seller may refuse
- Creates breach issue
- May delay closing

### Scenario 2: FHA to Conventional

**Why It Happens:**
- Buyer gets more funds
- Property has FHA issues
- Want faster closing

**Usually Acceptable:**
- Sellers generally okay with this
- No additional risk
- May expedite closing

### Scenario 3: Any Loan to Cash

**Why It Happens:**
- Financing fell through
- Buyer liquidated assets
- Gift funds available

**Best Scenario:**
- Seller almost always accepts
- Removes financing contingency
- Fastest closing

## Documentation Requirements

### In the Contract

**Paragraph for Loan Type:**
- Select correct box
- Specify loan program
- Note any special conditions

### What to Document

| Item | Where |
|------|-------|
| Loan Type | Contract checkbox |
| Loan Amount | Contract |
| Interest Rate | Loan contingency |
| Points | If applicable |
| Commitment Date | Contract/Rider |

---

## Handling Loan Type Disputes

### If Seller Discovers Switch

**Steps:**
1. Acknowledge the change
2. Explain circumstances
3. Demonstrate buyer still qualifies
4. Negotiate if needed
5. Document any agreement

### Potential Resolutions

**Options:**
- Seller agrees to new loan type
- Price adjustment
- Credit adjustment
- Contract termination (worst case)

---

## Agent Liability Considerations

### Protect Yourself

**Best Practices:**
- Document loan type discussions
- Confirm buyer understanding
- Note any changes in writing
- Keep communication records

### What NOT to Do

- Don't misrepresent loan type
- Don't promise without buyer confirmation
- Don't hide loan changes
- Don't assume seller won't notice

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Loan Type Affects Acceptance:** Sellers choose based on loan program
2. **Switching Creates Risk:** May constitute breach of contract
3. **Disclose Changes:** Never hide loan program changes
4. **Use Attorney Language:** Flexibility provisions help
5. **Communicate Early:** If switch needed, discuss with all parties
