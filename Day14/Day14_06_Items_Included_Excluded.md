# Day 14-06: Items Included and Excluded

## Overview

The items included and excluded section of the contract is often handled poorly, with agents simply writing "as per MLS." This creates problems because attorneys typically don't consider MLS part of the contract. Specific, detailed lists are essential.

---

## The "As Per MLS" Problem

### Why It's Insufficient

> "Nine times out of 10 attorneys say the MLS is not part of the contract. My client didn't write it. I don't want those representations to be included."

**Attorney's Position:**
- MLS is marketing material
- Seller's agent wrote it, not seller
- Attorney doesn't want client bound by it
- May refuse to include MLS representations

### The Buyer's Counter-Argument

> "As a seller, you worked with your agent, you put that information out there in the public - why can't I rely on it?"

**For Buyer's Agent:**
- Fight to include MLS references
- But also list items specifically
- Don't rely solely on MLS

---

## What to Specifically List

### High-Tech and Modern Items

> "If there's something very specific, especially in these newer homes... the Nest system... the WiFi fridge... additional appliances... if there's something very specific beyond your ordinary appliances."

**Examples to List:**
- Smart home systems (Nest, Ring, Alexa)
- Smart appliances
- Home automation
- Electric car chargers
- Built-in speakers/audio
- Security systems

### Commonly Disputed Items

| Item | Issue |
|------|-------|
| Washer/Dryer | Often assumed included but taken |
| Light Fixtures | Seller may want to keep special ones |
| Window Treatments | Blinds vs. curtains dispute |
| TV Mounts | Are they "attached"? |
| Outdoor Equipment | Grills, furniture |
| Flooring | Yes, someone tried to take deck flooring |

### The Deck Flooring Story

> "Somebody actually tried to take flooring one time... it was flooring on a deck surface and they were like 'well you know it's removable.'"

**Lesson:** If it could possibly be disputed, list it specifically.

---

## Light Fixture Rules

### Seller Taking Fixtures

**The Rule:**
> "If there are light fixtures the seller wants to take... fine, but they're required to replace them with what we call builder's grade - just standard fixtures before they go."

**What This Means:**
- Seller can take special chandelier
- Must replace with basic fixture
- Cannot leave empty socket
- Cannot just leave a light bulb

### Negotiating Fixtures

**If Seller Wants to Keep:**
1. List specific fixtures to be removed
2. Note replacement requirement
3. Consider asking for credit instead
4. Factor into price negotiation

---

## Personal Property vs. Real Property

### What's Typically Included

**Real Property (Stays):**
- Built-in appliances
- Attached fixtures
- Built-in cabinetry
- Ceiling fans
- Garage door openers
- Window blinds

**Personal Property (Goes):**
- Furniture
- Decorations
- Portable appliances
- Potted plants
- Removable rugs

### The Grey Area

**Items That Cause Disputes:**
- Refrigerator (plugged in but not built-in)
- Washer/Dryer (hooked up but movable)
- Area rugs (over hardwood)
- Mounted TVs (brackets attached)
- Outdoor furniture (on deck)

---

## Furniture and Personal Items

### Attorney's Position

> "Attorneys don't like to get involved in that. You're paying a purchase price for the property itself, not for all the furniture."

**Issues with Including Furniture:**
- Transfer tax complications
- Value attribution problems
- Separate from real estate transaction
- May affect lender review

### How to Handle Furniture Sales

**Option 1: Side Agreement**
- Keep separate from contract
- Direct transaction between parties
- No attorney involvement needed

**Option 2: Collective List**
> "Sometimes we'll just come to a collective list and make sure we're all on the same page about it but don't really include it necessarily in the contract."

**Key Point:** Don't include furniture in purchase price.

---

## Seller's Disclosure Cross-Reference

### The Conflict Issue

**Problem Scenario:**
> "During the... seller's disclosure... it'll ask 'are these things in working order' and they check yes washer and dryer. On the contract they said they're not included."

**What Happens:**
- Disclosure says "working order" - yes
- Contract says "not included"
- Buyer assumes it's staying
- Dispute at closing

### How to Handle

> "Clarify that they just hit that it's working... it's working but it's not included."

**Best Practice:**
- Review disclosure against contract
- Note any discrepancies
- Clarify in writing
- Address in attorney review if needed

---

## Best Practices for Listing Items

### For Buyer's Agent

**When Writing Offer:**
1. List all items buyer expects
2. Reference MLS AND list specifically
3. Include special/high-value items
4. Note anything discussed verbally
5. Err on side of over-inclusion

### For Listing Agent

**When Taking Listing:**
1. Clarify with seller what stays/goes
2. List accurately in MLS
3. Note any items seller keeping
4. Discuss replacements for removed items
5. Document in writing

### Template Language

**Include:**
```
All appliances currently installed including refrigerator,
stove, dishwasher, microwave, washer, and dryer. All window
treatments including blinds and curtains. Smart home system
including Nest thermostat and Ring doorbell. All light
fixtures currently installed.
```

**Exclude:**
```
Chandelier in formal dining room (to be replaced with
builder's grade fixture). Washer and dryer in basement.
Seller's personal furniture and decorations.
```

## Attorney Review Expectations

### What Tony Does

> "Especially if there's things in the listing agreement I'm going to want to actually relist them in attorney review and make sure we're very clear that they're included or not included."

**In Rider:**
- Repeats included items specifically
- Lists excluded items
- Clarifies any disputes
- Addresses any MLS discrepancies

### Information Attorney Needs

**From Contract:**
- Items listed as included
- Items listed as excluded
- Any special notes

**From MLS:**
- What was marketed as included
- Property features noted
- Any special equipment mentioned

---

## Common Disputes and Resolutions

### Dispute 1: "MLS Said It Was Included"

**Situation:** Item in MLS, not in contract

**Resolution Options:**
- Negotiate inclusion
- Price adjustment
- Seller agrees to leave it
- Buyer accepts exclusion

### Dispute 2: Seller Changed Mind

**Real Example:**
> "The seller's like 'yeah no, too bad, I'm taking it'... on both sides [it] was included, it's in the MLS... No, we're taking it."

**Resolution:**
- Stand firm on contract
- Negotiate if needed
- Make decision on deal
- Document everything

### Dispute 3: What's "Attached"?

**Question:** Is a wall-mounted TV attached?

**Arguments:**
- Buyer: Bracket is attached to wall
- Seller: TV itself is personal property

**Best Practice:** Specify in contract

## Key Takeaways

1. **Don't Rely on MLS Alone:** List items specifically in contract
2. **Be Detailed:** Better to over-specify than assume
3. **Fixtures Must Be Replaced:** If seller takes, must replace with builder's grade
4. **Furniture Separate:** Keep outside main contract
5. **Cross-Reference Disclosure:** Check for conflicts
6. **Attorney Will Verify:** Rider will clarify included/excluded
