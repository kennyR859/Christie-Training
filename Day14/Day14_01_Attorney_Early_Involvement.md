# Day 14-01: Attorney Early Involvement

## Overview

The single most important tip for smooth attorney review is getting an attorney involved as early as possible in the transaction process. This proactive approach prevents misunderstandings, protects clients, and streamlines the entire closing process.

---

## Why Early Involvement Matters

### The Core Problem

> "Probably nine times out of 10 when I have an offer like that come to me in attorney review, the client doesn't actually understand what they waived."

When attorneys are brought in late:
- Clients have already agreed to terms they don't understand
- Offers contain language that doesn't protect the client
- Creative deal structures lack proper legal framework
- Time pressure causes mistakes and oversights

### The Solution

> "Get an attorney involved as early as possible in the process with your clients. That is going to be the biggest help for you in getting your offers through and also in getting through attorney review smoothly."

---

## Benefits of Early Attorney Involvement

### 1. Crafting Better Offers

**What Attorneys Can Help With:**
- Getting language correct in offers
- Structuring creative deals properly
- Ensuring contingencies are worded correctly
- Making offers more appealing while still protecting client

**Example - Appraisal Waiver:**
- Agent wants to waive appraisal to make offer attractive
- Attorney ensures client understands financial implications
- Language is precise and legally sound
- Client can make informed decision

### 2. Client Education

**First-Time Buyers Especially Benefit:**
- Tons of questions answered upfront
- Process explained before stress of contract
- Nuances explained (like tax reassessments)
- Fewer surprises during transaction

**Example - Renovated Home:**
> "You might not know that next year there's going to be an added assessment on the taxes... their property taxes are going to increase significantly. We don't want the buyers to find that out after closing."

### 3. Smoother Attorney Review

**When Client Is Already Educated:**
- No "I didn't agree to that" moments
- Faster rider completion
- Fewer back-and-forth negotiations
- Less risk of deal falling apart

---

## Practical Implementation

### For Buyer's Agents

**Step 1: Build Attorney Relationships**
- Identify 1-2 attorneys you trust
- Develop ongoing relationship
- Use them for general questions, not just transactions

**Step 2: Early Introduction**
- Introduce buyer to attorney BEFORE offer accepted
- No payment required at introduction
- Attorney can onboard client with process information

**Step 3: Offer Process Collaboration**
- Consult attorney on creative offer structures
- Verify client understands contingency waivers
- Get language reviewed before submission

### For Listing Agents

**Step 1: Seller Preparation**
- Have seller consult attorney about disclosure requirements
- Address potential issues (CO problems, title issues) early
- Understand what seller is obligated to provide

**Step 2: Deal Structuring**
- Understand legal implications of terms you're requesting
- Know what's negotiable in attorney review
- Prepare for common attorney objections

---

## The Retainer Process

### Tony's Approach

> "I have a retainer agreement. It acts like a guide letter for the closing. There's lots of information in there for the clients."

**What the Retainer Process Includes:**
- Retainer agreement and fee
- Introduction to the closing process
- What to expect from attorney review to closing
- Timeline of key events

### Timing

- Best completed before offer accepted
- Not required to hold up attorney review if needed
- Having it done early prevents last-minute rushing

---

## Building Attorney Relationships

### Characteristics of Good Working Relationships

> "I work very collaboratively with my agents. You know, we become very friendly. I treat it as a team everybody has the same goal in mind."

**What to Look For:**
- Attorney willing to answer general questions
- Collaborative approach rather than adversarial
- Available for consultation during offer process
- Clear communication style

### How Agents Support Attorneys

**Information Sharing:**
- Communicate special deal terms
- Explain property characteristics
- Note any negotiated concessions
- Provide complete document packages

**Responsiveness:**
- Answer attorney questions promptly
- Available during attorney review period
- Help gather missing information
- Track and verify deadlines

---

## Common Situations Requiring Early Attorney Input

### 1. Sale Contingencies

If offer is contingent on client selling their home:
- Parameters must be clearly defined
- Timeline for sale
- What happens if sale doesn't occur
- How closing dates coordinate

### 2. Tenant-Occupied Properties

Multiple questions need answers:
- How many units occupied
- Lease terms and duration
- Accepting with tenants or vacant
- Notice to quit requirements

### 3. Creative Financing

Any non-standard financing:
- Appraisal waivers (full or partial)
- Seller concession requests
- Unusual loan programs
- Cash with financing contingency

### 4. Property Issues

Known issues that need addressing:
- CO problems
- Open permits
- Title issues
- Boundary disputes

## The Team Approach

### Everyone Has Same Goal

> "Everybody has the same goal in mind... it's a group effort."

**The Team:**
- Buyer and their agent
- Seller and their agent
- Buyer's attorney
- Seller's attorney
- Lender
- Title company

### Communication Is Key

> "I can't advise my client. They can't make informed decisions if I don't have all the information."

**Information Flow:**
- Agents provide deal context to attorneys
- Attorneys explain legal implications to clients
- Everyone stays informed of changes
- No surprises for any party

---

## What NOT to Do

### Don't Wait Until Attorney Review

**Problems with Late Involvement:**
- Client already committed to terms
- Language may be incorrect or unclear
- No time for proper client education
- Pressure to rush rider completion

### Don't Assume Client Understands

> "The client doesn't actually understand what they waived... all of a sudden you have this offer out there and the client saying whoa whoa whoa I didn't agree to that."

**Always Verify:**
- Client understands financial implications
- Client knows what they're waiving
- Client is comfortable with risks
- Client has had opportunity to ask questions

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Earliest is Best:** Introduce attorney before offer, not after acceptance

2. **Education Prevents Problems:** Informed clients make better decisions

3. **Team Approach Works:** Collaboration beats adversarial relationships

4. **Use Attorney as Resource:** Questions during offer process are welcome

5. **Complete Information:** Give attorneys everything they need upfront
