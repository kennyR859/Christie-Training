# Day 14-07: Time of Essence and Contract Dates

## Overview

"Time of Essence" is a legal concept that makes deadlines absolute. In New Jersey real estate, attorneys routinely delete standard time of essence provisions from contracts, with one important exception: the closing date can be made time of essence through a formal notice process.

---

## What "Time of Essence" Means

### Legal Definition

> "If a date is time of essence, what that means is it's absolutely due today. There's no delays, no extensions. If you miss this due date, you're screwed. You've waived it or the contract is canceled."

**Consequences of Missing Time of Essence Date:**
- Rights may be waived
- Contract may be canceled
- Damages may be owed
- No opportunity to cure

### Why It's Significant

**Normal Contract Dates:**
- Provide guidance for timeline
- Generally allow reasonable delays
- Can be extended by agreement
- Missing doesn't automatically end contract

**Time of Essence Dates:**
- Absolute deadlines
- No flexibility
- Failure has immediate consequences
- Cannot be extended without consent

---

## Standard Attorney Practice

### Paragraph 8 Deletion

> "We usually delete this whole paragraph... we do not make the contract due dates time of essence."

**What Gets Deleted:**
- Time of essence language for all dates
- First and last paragraphs of Paragraph 8
- Absolute deadline requirements

**What Gets Kept:**
> "The middle one says that all the dates run from when attorney review is concluded - that's legitimate."

### Why Attorneys Delete It

**Practical Reasons:**
- Transactions rarely go exactly as planned
- Delays happen for legitimate reasons
- Punishing minor delays is counterproductive
- Notice and cure is fairer

**Legal Protection:**
- Clients don't accidentally forfeit rights
- Time to remedy issues
- More equitable for both parties

---

## Contract Language Evolution

### Historical Context

> "That was never in that contract years ago. Time is of the essence. Then I don't remember how many years ago, maybe seven or eight years ago, they put that in and I have no idea why."

**Current Trend:**
> "They're getting better because I've noticed different provisions have really started conforming to what we normally do now."

### Standard Form vs. Practice

**What Contract Says:** All dates are time of essence
**What Attorneys Do:** Delete that language
**Result:** Dates are goals, not absolute deadlines

---

## The One Exception: Closing Date

### How Closing Becomes Time of Essence

**Process:**
1. Original closing date passes
2. Party ready to close serves Time of Essence Notice
3. Notice specifies new closing date
4. That new date IS time of essence

### Notice Requirements

> "Usually by law it's 14 days, but usually attorneys agree to make it a 10-day notice."

**Standard Process:**
| Step | Timeline |
|------|----------|
| Original closing date | Passes |
| TOE Notice sent | Day 0 |
| New TOE date | Day 10-14 |

### Effect of Time of Essence Notice

> "Everybody has to be ready, willing, and able to close by that date. If they don't close, then the person who sent the notice is entitled to cancel the contract and hold the other party liable for damages."

**Requirements to Close:**
- Funds must be ready
- All documents prepared
- Clear title
- Lender ready to fund
- Both parties available

---

## What Dates Are NOT Time of Essence

### Inspection Period

**Standard Practice:**
- X days to complete inspections
- If not done, typically can request extension
- Missing date doesn't cancel contract
- Subject to attorney review rider

### Mortgage Commitment Date

**Standard Practice:**
- Target date for commitment letter
- Lender delays are common
- Extensions typically granted
- Not automatic contract termination

### Attorney Review Period

**Standard Practice:**
- 3 business days in NJ
- Can be extended by agreement
- Missing doesn't automatically approve contract
- Riders can be sent late with agreement

---

## Notice and Cure System

### How It Works Instead

**When Date Is Missed:**
1. Non-performing party notified
2. Given opportunity to cure
3. Reasonable time to perform
4. Only then consequences

**Benefits:**
- Prevents unfair results
- Allows for legitimate delays
- Maintains deal when possible
- Protects both parties

---

## Practical Implications for Agents

### What This Means Day-to-Day

**Contract Dates Are:**
- Guidelines for scheduling
- Target dates for planning
- Not automatic termination points
- Subject to reasonable extensions

**Exception:**
- Closing date CAN become absolute
- Only through formal notice
- After original date passed

### Setting Expectations

**For Clients:**
- "This is our target closing date"
- "Dates can be adjusted if needed"
- "Both parties need to be reasonable"
- "If issues arise, we communicate"

**For Yourself:**
- Track all dates carefully
- Meet deadlines when possible
- Communicate early about delays
- Document everything

## Time of Essence Notice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Buyer Delays

**Situation:**
- Closing date passes
- Buyer's lender not ready
- Seller ready to close

**Seller's Options:**
1. Wait and accommodate
2. Serve TOE notice
3. Set new absolute date
4. Prepare to cancel if not met

### Scenario 2: Seller Delays

**Situation:**
- Closing date passes
- Seller hasn't cleared title issues
- Buyer ready to close

**Buyer's Options:**
1. Wait for resolution
2. Serve TOE notice
3. Set deadline for performance
4. Prepare to cancel if not met

### Scenario 3: Both Parties Ready

**Situation:**
- Closing date approaches
- Minor scheduling conflict
- Everyone wants to close

**Solution:**
- Simply reschedule
- No TOE notice needed
- Cooperative approach

---

## Damages and Consequences

### If TOE Date Missed

**Party Who Sent Notice Can:**
- Cancel contract
- Retain/pursue deposit
- Seek damages

**Potential Damages:**
- Carrying costs
- Lost opportunity
- Storage costs
- Additional expenses

### Defending Against TOE

**Valid Defenses:**
- Other party not ready
- Title issues from seller
- Notice improperly served
- Reasonable excuse

---

## Best Practices

### For Agents

1. **Track All Dates:** Even if not TOE
2. **Communicate Early:** If deadline will be missed
3. **Document Extensions:** Get in writing
4. **Understand TOE Process:** Know when it applies
5. **Coordinate with Attorney:** Before any notice

### For Timeline Management

**Set Realistic Dates:**
- Account for typical delays
- Build in buffer time
- Understand lender timelines
- Know municipality processing times

**Monitor Progress:**
- Regular check-ins with all parties
- Early warning on delays
- Proactive problem-solving

## Key Terminology

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Time of Essence | Date is absolute deadline |
| Notice and Cure | Opportunity to fix before consequences |
| TOE Notice | Formal notice making date absolute |
| Ready, Willing, Able | All conditions met to perform |
| Breach | Failure to perform obligations |

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Most Dates NOT Time of Essence:** Contract dates are targets, not absolutes
2. **Attorneys Delete TOE Language:** Standard practice in NJ
3. **Closing Date Exception:** Can become TOE through notice
4. **Notice Required:** 10-14 days typically
5. **Consequences Are Serious:** Damages and cancellation possible
