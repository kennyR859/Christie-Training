# Day 14-02: Appraisal Waiver Strategy

## Overview

Appraisal contingency waivers are one of the most commonly misunderstood aspects of real estate offers. This topic covers exactly what appraisal waivers mean, the financial implications, and how to properly advise clients.

---

## The Common Misunderstanding

### The Problem

> "Probably nine times out of 10 when I have an offer like that come to me in attorney review, the client doesn't actually understand what they waived."

**What Clients Often Think:**
- "We won't have an appraisal"
- "The appraisal doesn't matter"
- "We'll just pay what we offered"

**The Reality:**
- Appraisal is ALWAYS done if there's a lender
- Waiver affects buyer's financial obligations
- Can require significant additional cash

---

## How Appraisals Actually Work

### The Loan-to-Value Ratio

**Key Concept:** Lenders loan based on APPRAISED VALUE, not purchase price.

**Example Calculation:**

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Purchase Price | $1,000,000 |
| Loan-to-Value Ratio | 80% |
| Expected Mortgage | $800,000 |
| Expected Down Payment | $200,000 |

**If Appraisal Comes Back Lower:**

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Appraised Value | $900,000 |
| 80% of Appraised Value | $720,000 |
| Lender Will Loan | $720,000 |
| Gap (Buyer Must Cover) | $80,000 |
| **Total Cash Needed** | **$280,000** |

### The Pre-Approval Misconception

> "The pre-approvals are provided based on the purchase price... if that property now comes out to appraisal at $900,000 the lender is only going to loan you 80% of the appraised value, not the actual purchase price."

**What the Approval Letter Says:**
- Approved for $800,000 mortgage
- Based on $1,000,000 purchase price

**What Actually Happens:**
- Lender uses APPRAISED value
- If lower, loan amount is lower
- Buyer must make up the difference

---

## Types of Appraisal Waivers

### 1. Full Appraisal Waiver

**What It Means:**
- Buyer waives ANY deficiency in appraised value
- Buyer pays full purchase price regardless of appraisal
- Buyer must cover entire gap in cash

**Risk Level:** HIGHEST

**Example:**
- Purchase price: $1,000,000
- Appraisal: $850,000
- Gap buyer must cover: $150,000 additional cash

### 2. Partial Waiver (Listing to Offer Price)

**What It Means:**
- Waive gap between listing price and offer price
- If appraises at or above listing price, buyer is fine
- If below listing price, can still negotiate

**Risk Level:** MODERATE

**Example:**
- Listing price: $900,000
- Offer/Purchase price: $975,000
- Appraisal: $900,000
- Gap buyer waived: $75,000

> "From a seller's perspective I think that's fair because... I told you what I thought the house was worth. You're offering more, so why should I lose out if it appraises for what I told you it was worth."

### 3. Capped Waiver

**What It Means:**
- Buyer will cover gap UP TO specified amount
- Beyond that amount, contingency kicks in
- Buyer has defined maximum exposure

**Risk Level:** CONTROLLED

**Example:**
- Purchase price: $1,000,000
- Buyer waives up to: $50,000 gap
- Appraisal: $920,000
- Gap: $80,000
- Buyer covers: $50,000
- Remaining $30,000: negotiable or cancel

## Agent's Role and Liability

### What Agents Should Do

> "A lot of the times things already come to me that you know they've been kind of already offered... I 100% agree with you, you can recommend it... but on the other hand you need to be cautious about what you're urging or pushing or recommending."

**Best Practice:**
1. **Suggest, don't recommend:** Present as option, not advice
2. **Explain prevalence:** "Many buyers are doing this"
3. **Emphasize understanding:** Client MUST understand implications
4. **Defer to attorney:** Get help with correct language

### The Scary Example

> "There was one gal in a class many many months ago... she thought there would be no appraisal and her client thought the same thing. Thank God she didn't get the offer."

**Why This Matters:**
- Agent could have been liable
- Client would have been in impossible situation
- Deal would have fallen apart badly

---

## Client Education Script

### Questions to Ask Client

1. "Do you understand that the appraisal will still happen?"
2. "Do you understand the bank loans based on appraised value, not purchase price?"
3. "If there's a $50,000 gap, do you have $50,000 extra to cover it?"
4. "Are you prepared to potentially be underwater from day one?"

### Financial Verification

**Before Agreeing to Waiver:**
- Verify client has liquid assets to cover potential gap
- Get specific amount they can cover
- Consider capped waiver based on available funds

---

## When Appraisals Go Wrong

### Appealing an Appraisal

> "There is availability to appeal an appraisal... a little longer, it's really not always successful."

**When to Consider Appeal:**
- Appraiser missed features (bathroom, bedroom)
- Appraiser unfamiliar with neighborhood
- Clear errors in comparable selection
- Obvious calculation mistakes

**Who Can Help:**
- Realtors know property characteristics
- Clients know improvements made
- Agents know neighborhood nuances

### Switching Lenders

**Real Example:**
> "We got an appraiser that was notorious for screwing things up in a specific neighborhood... this was an appraiser from South Jersey... they had to switch lenders halfway through."

**When Necessary:**
- Appraiser has known issues in area
- Appeal not possible or unsuccessful
- Significant appraisal discrepancy
- Time permits lender change

### Neighborhood Knowledge Matters

> "If you have a little Cape Cod in the lawns it's not going to sell for the same price as the Cape Cod in the Heights or the Country Club section, and that appraiser has to know that."

**Agent's Role:**
- Attend appraisal with information
- Provide relevant comparable sales
- Explain neighborhood sections
- Note unique property features

---

## Negotiation After Low Appraisal

### Options Available

1. **Buyer Covers Gap:** Per waiver terms
2. **Price Reduction:** Seller reduces to appraised value
3. **Split the Difference:** Meet somewhere in middle
4. **Cancel Contract:** If contingency allows

> "That's what a good salesperson does - they are able to negotiate."

### Reality Check

Even with full waiver:
- Seller may negotiate rather than lose deal
- Repeat low appraisals likely with next buyer
- Sometimes meeting in middle makes sense

---

## Underwater Risk Explanation

### What "Underwater" Means

> "If you're already paying more than what your house is actually worth and God forbid we have another market churn or something, you're automatically underwater."

**Being Underwater:**
- Owe more than home is worth
- Can't sell without bringing money to closing
- Can't refinance easily
- Trapped if need to move

### Long-Term Perspective

**The Appraisal as Documentation:**
- Shows fair market value at purchase
- Reference point for future
- Important for financial planning

**Questions for Clients:**
- "How long do you plan to stay?"
- "Can you afford to wait out market correction?"
- "Is this your forever home or stepping stone?"

---

## Best Practices Summary

### For Buyer's Agents

1. **Never Assume Understanding:** Verify client gets it
2. **Use Attorney Early:** Get help with language
3. **Document Discussions:** Note client acknowledgment
4. **Verify Finances:** Confirm liquid assets available
5. **Consider Caps:** Suggest limited exposure when possible

### For Listing Agents

1. **Explain to Sellers:** Waiver benefits and limitations
2. **Set Expectations:** Low appraisal possible
3. **Prepare for Negotiation:** Even with waiver, may need flexibility
4. **Price Realistically:** Reduces appraisal gap risk

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Appraisal Always Happens:** Waiver doesn't eliminate appraisal
2. **Lenders Use Appraised Value:** Not purchase price
3. **Cash Required:** Gap must be covered in cash
4. **Multiple Waiver Types:** Full, partial, or capped
5. **Client Must Understand:** Financial implications are significant
6. **Agent Liability Exists:** Be careful how you present options
