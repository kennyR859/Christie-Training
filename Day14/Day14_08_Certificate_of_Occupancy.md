# Day 14-08: Certificate of Occupancy (CO) Requirements

## Overview

The Certificate of Occupancy (CO) is a town-issued document verifying that a property meets building codes and is safe for occupancy. The contract includes a liability cap for CO-related repairs, which is routinely adjusted by attorneys.

---

## What is a Certificate of Occupancy?

### Definition

A CO certifies that:
- Building meets local building codes
- All permits are closed
- No outstanding violations
- Property is safe to occupy

### When It's Required

**Before Closing:**
- Application made to municipality
- Town reviews permit records
- May conduct inspection
- Issues CO if all clear

### What Can Go Wrong

**Potential Issues:**
- Open permits (work never inspected)
- Violations found during inspection
- Work done without permits
- Illegal modifications

---

## The Liability Cap

### Standard Contract Language

> "The contract says 1.5% of the purchase price."

**Example on $500,000 Home:**
- Contract standard: $7,500 cap

### What Attorneys Actually Use

> "We never never do that. If it's a condo it's $500. Single family or more it's usually around $1,000."

**Standard Attorney Caps:**
| Property Type | Typical Cap |
|--------------|-------------|
| Condo | $500 |
| Single Family | $1,000 |
| Multi-Family | $1,000-$2,000 |

---

## Understanding the Cap

### What the Cap Means

**Seller's Obligation:**
- Pay up to cap amount for CO issues
- If costs exceed cap, can negotiate
- If can't agree, seller may cancel

### How It Works

**Under Cap:**
- Seller pays full cost
- Transaction proceeds

**Over Cap:**
- Negotiate cost sharing
- Or seller can cancel
- Buyer may agree to pay excess

### Why Caps Exist

> "From a seller's perspective, if they're required by the town to make some improvements to the property that the buyer's going to get the use and benefit of after closing, maybe they don't want to be responsible for payment of everything."

---

## Common CO Issues

### Illegal Modifications

> "If there's an extra kitchen in the basement that's not allowed, they might have to rip everything out. They might have to seal up that bathroom - that happens a lot, especially down in Hudson County."

**Examples:**
- Basement kitchens
- Unpermitted bathrooms
- Illegal apartments
- Unapproved additions

### Open Permits

**Situation:**
- Work was done
- Permit was pulled
- Final inspection never done

**Resolution:**
- Schedule inspection
- Make any required corrections
- Get permit closed

### Violations

**Types:**
- Code violations
- Safety issues
- Structural concerns
- Electrical/plumbing problems

---

## Buyer vs. Seller Perspectives

### Buyer's Position

> "Sometimes you go into it and you feel like, well, that's the seller's problem. The condition of the property is the seller's problem. They have to give me a CO and say we're good to go."

**Buyer's Argument:**
- Seller owns property
- Seller responsible for condition
- Seller should fix everything

### Seller's Position

**Seller's Argument:**
- Didn't know about issues
- Buyer gets benefit of repairs
- Cap provides reasonable limit
- May choose to cancel vs. over-pay

### Reality

Both have valid points - caps provide compromise.

---

## How to Handle CO Issues

### For Buyer's Agents

**Strategy:**
- May leave cap blank (argue for higher limit)
- Push for seller responsibility
- Negotiate if issues found
- Consider property condition

**When to Leave Blank:**
> "I would say if you're... representing the buyer, then sometimes you leave it blank."

### For Listing Agents

**Pre-Listing Due Diligence:**
1. Check for open permits
2. Identify any violations
3. Research unpermitted work
4. Disclose known issues

**Get Ahead of Issues:**
- File CO application early
- Find out what's outstanding
- Address before listing if possible

---

## Municipality Variations

### What Towns Do

**Some Towns:**
- Always do inspections
- Thorough review process
- May find issues

**Other Towns:**
- Desk review only
- Check records, no inspection
- Faster process

### Unknown Issues

**The Risk:**
- Seller may not know about problems
- Previous owner's work without permits
- Town finds during CO inspection
- Surprise costs

---

## Real-World Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Minor Issue

**Situation:**
- CO inspection finds no GFCI outlets in bathroom
- Cost to fix: $200

**Resolution:**
- Well under cap
- Seller pays, transaction proceeds

### Scenario 2: Major Issue

**Situation:**
- Basement apartment is illegal
- Must be removed
- Cost: $15,000

**Resolution:**
- Far exceeds typical cap
- Negotiate: Seller pays cap, buyer pays rest
- Or: Price reduction
- Or: Seller cancels

### Scenario 3: Hidden History

**Tony's Example:**
> "I even found something - I put a hot water heater in and I didn't realize that the building inspector never came. The builder never called and I had something... never called to get the building inspector to OK."

**Lesson:** Even attorneys have open permits they don't know about.

---

## Getting Ahead of CO Issues

### For Listing Agents

> "If you're going to focus primarily on listings, it's great to get ahead of things on your side. File the CO application immediately. Call the building department. Find out what's outstanding."

**Steps:**
1. Go to municipality
2. Request permit history
3. Identify open permits
4. Schedule inspections
5. Resolve before listing

### For Buyer's Agents

**Due Diligence:**
- Review permit history in OPRA
- Ask about known issues
- Factor into offer strategy
- Prepare for surprises

---

## Contract Language

### Standard Provisions

**What Contract Says:**
- Seller must obtain CO
- Cap on liability (1.5%)
- Process for excess costs

**What Rider Says:**
- Reduced cap ($500-$1,000)
- Specific procedures
- Negotiation process
- Cancellation rights

### Key Terms to Understand

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| CO Cap | Maximum seller must spend |
| Open Permit | Work not inspected |
| Violation | Code non-compliance |
| C of C | Certificate of Continued Occupancy |

---

## Timeline Considerations

### CO Processing Time

**Variables:**
- Municipality efficiency
- Inspection requirements
- Issue discovery
- Repair completion

### Planning Ahead

**Best Practice:**
- Apply for CO immediately upon listing
- Don't wait until under contract
- Address issues proactively
- Build time into closing timeline

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **CO Required:** Most transactions need CO before closing
2. **Cap Is Negotiable:** Standard contract vs. attorney practice differ
3. **Typical Caps:** $500 (condo), $1,000 (single family)
4. **Issues Common:** Open permits, violations, illegal work
5. **Get Ahead:** File early, address issues proactively
6. **Buyer Benefits:** Repairs improve property buyer receives
