# Day 14-15: OPRA Requests and Agent Responsibility

## Overview

OPRA (Open Public Records Act) requests provide valuable property information, but agents must understand a critical principle: **the more information you have, the more you're responsible for.** Sending OPRA requests yourself may create liability you didn't intend.

---

## The Core Principle

### Information = Responsibility

> "If you're sending out an OPRA, you're now responsible for the information you received."

**The Risk:**
- Agent sends OPRA request
- Receives documents
- Doesn't fully review
- Something important missed
- Problem discovered after closing
- Agent had information but didn't communicate

### Legal Exposure

> "The minute you come into contact with that information, you're responsible for it."

**What This Means:**
- You received the information
- You should have reviewed it
- You should have communicated relevant findings
- Failure to do so = potential liability

---

## The Better Approach

### Let Others Handle It

> "Just leave it to the attorney. Do the OPRA. Or advise the client that they should do the OPRA themselves or go to the municipality."

**Options:**
1. **Attorney handles OPRA** - Their job to review and advise
2. **Client does OPRA** - Their information, their responsibility
3. **Direct to municipality** - Client gets firsthand

### Why This Works Better

**Agent Stays Clean:**
- No information to be responsible for
- Attorney handles legal review
- Client makes informed decisions
- Appropriate expertise applied

---

## What OPRA Provides

### Common OPRA Requests

**Information Available:**
- Building permits (open and closed)
- Certificate of Occupancy history
- Violations
- Municipal assessments
- Zoning information
- Tax information
- Environmental records

### Contract Schedule

> "There's a great schedule to the contract that says what you should request in an OPRA."

**The Schedule Lists:**
- Standard items to request
- Guidance for comprehensive review
- Checklist for due diligence

### How to Use the Schedule

**Best Practice:**
- Give schedule to client
- "Here's what you should request"
- "Go to the municipality"
- "Get all the information you need"

---

## Real Example of Risk

### Scenario: Tax Discrepancy

**What Could Happen:**
> "If something goes wrong down the line and we find out 'well the taxes were this but they're actually this' and you had a document from Day 1 that said that - why didn't you tell anybody? Why didn't you explain that? Why didn't you read it?"

**The Problem:**
- Agent got tax information
- Document showed different amount
- Agent didn't notice or communicate
- Buyer relied on wrong information
- Agent had the right information

### The Liability

**Questions Asked:**
- Did agent receive the document?
- Did agent review it?
- Did agent communicate findings?
- Did agent understand what it said?

---

## Being Helpful vs. Being Liable

### The Balance

> "Always caution agents - very good to be helpful, but again, another reason why it's important to work closely with your attorney."

**Helpful:** Telling client to get OPRA done
**Liable:** Doing OPRA and not reviewing thoroughly

### Where to Draw the Line

**Do:**
- Recommend OPRA to clients
- Provide OPRA schedule
- Connect client with municipality
- Work with attorney on review

**Don't:**
- Send OPRA requests yourself
- Receive documents without reviewing
- Assume you understand legal documents
- Take on attorney's role

---

## Types of Documents That Create Risk

### Complex Documents

**Beyond Agent Expertise:**
- Permit details
- Violation notices
- Legal descriptions
- Easement documents
- Environmental reports

### Why They're Risky

**Even If You Read Them:**
- May not understand implications
- May miss important details
- Legal interpretation required
- Technical knowledge needed

### Example: Airport Safety Zone

> "We thought there was this whole catastrophe that there, you know, this property was subject to an airport safety zone. All of a sudden I had to become an expert on the airport safety zone. And then when it was discussed in attorney review - 'oh that was checked on accident.'"

**Lesson:** Even what looks like a problem may not be - or vice versa. Expert review needed.

---

## Proper Information Flow

### Best Practice Process

```
Client Needs Information
        ↓
Agent Recommends OPRA
        ↓
Client/Attorney Obtains Documents
        ↓
Attorney Reviews
        ↓
Attorney Advises Client
        ↓
Informed Decision Made
```

### Why This Works

**Agent Role:**
- Facilitator, not investigator
- Connector, not analyzer
- Advisor on process, not content

**Attorney Role:**
- Legal review
- Interpretation
- Client advice
- Liability for legal matters

---

## When Agents DO Get Information

### Sometimes Unavoidable

**Situations:**
- MLS information received
- Seller provides documents
- Information comes unsolicited
- Part of normal transaction flow

### How to Handle

**When You Receive Information:**
1. Forward to attorney
2. Note that you received it
3. Don't interpret
4. Let expert review
5. Document the handoff

---

## Protecting Yourself

### Documentation

**Keep Records Of:**
- What you told client about OPRA
- That you recommended attorney review
- When you forwarded documents
- What you did NOT review

### Communication

**Sample Script:**
> "I recommend you request these records from the municipality. Here's the list of what to ask for. Your attorney should review whatever you receive to make sure everything is in order."

### Referral

**To Attorney:**
> "I received these documents from the town. I haven't reviewed them in detail - that's your expertise. Please review and advise the client."

---

## The Listing Agent Exception

### Getting Ahead of Issues

**For Listing Agents:**
- May need to check permit status
- CO application requires knowledge
- Different relationship with seller

### Tony's Advice on Getting Ahead

> "As a listing agent, go to the town and find out what's outstanding."

**Note:** This is checking STATUS, not doing full legal review.

### What's Appropriate

**OK for Listing Agent:**
- Check if permits are open
- Ask about CO requirements
- Identify obvious issues
- Work with seller to resolve

**Still Attorney's Job:**
- Legal interpretation
- Risk assessment
- Document review
- Client advice

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Information = Responsibility:** What you know, you're accountable for
2. **Let Others Request:** Attorney or client should do OPRA
3. **Use the Schedule:** Contract has list of what to request
4. **Forward, Don't Interpret:** Pass documents to attorney
5. **Document Your Process:** Show what you did and didn't do
6. **Be Helpful Appropriately:** Guide process, don't take over
