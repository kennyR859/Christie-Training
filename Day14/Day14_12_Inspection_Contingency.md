# Day 14-12: Inspection Contingency

## Overview

The inspection contingency section of the contract is almost always completely rewritten in attorney review. Understanding how attorneys handle inspections helps agents manage client expectations and facilitate smoother negotiations.

---

## Standard Attorney Practice

### Complete Rewrite

> "Most of the time we completely rewrite this entire contingency in attorney review. It's usually based on what you guys put in the notes, what's negotiated."

**Why Rewritten:**
- Contract language doesn't reflect negotiations
- Standard form doesn't protect client
- Specific exclusions needed
- Timeline needs adjustment

### What Gets Changed

- Inspection timeframe
- What's covered
- Exclusions
- Response procedures
- Resolution process

---

## The "Structural, Environmental, Mechanical, and Safety" Trick

### What It Looks Like

> "Inspections for structural, environmental, mechanical, and safety only."

### What It Actually Means

> "That's an inspection. That's a full inspection. But I love it because you get away with looking like you're limiting something."

**Reality Check:**
- Structural = Foundation, framing, roof
- Environmental = Mold, radon, lead
- Mechanical = HVAC, plumbing, electrical
- Safety = Everything that could harm someone

**In Other Words:** This covers everything important.

---

## Material Defects Standard

### Legal Definition

> "Material defects is actually defined by a statute. It's something that substantially affects the safety, value, or habitability of the dwelling."

### What It Includes

**Safety:** Anything that could cause harm
**Value:** Significant repair costs
**Habitability:** Affects ability to live there

### What It Excludes

> "It does not include cosmetic or stylistic elements."

**Not Material Defects:**
- Paint color
- Dated fixtures
- Style preferences
- Minor wear and tear

---

## Standard Seller Exclusions

### 1. Systems Beyond Useful Life

> "Every seller side is going to exclude systems that are beyond their useful life but otherwise in working order."

**How It Works:**
- Water heater: 8-year expected life, now 12 years old
- Still working properly
- Inspector notes age
- NOT a defect requiring repair

**Key Phrase:** "Beyond useful life but otherwise in working order."

**What IS Required:**
- If there's an actual defect (broken, leaking, not functioning)
- Must be repaired regardless of age

### 2. Upgrades

> "A lot of sellers also exclude upgrades. Be a little careful with that because there could be something that's deemed an upgrade but is required for code compliance."

**The GFCI Example:**
> "GFCI outlets - crazy, they cost like $150 to update and replace. By code they're required. Their safety items are required anywhere that there's going to be water."

**Best Practice:**
- Accept upgrade exclusion BUT
- Carve out: "Unless it's a safety defect"

### 3. Maintenance Recommendations

> "Items that are recommended by the home inspector for maintenance but are not actually material defects."

**Examples:**
- "Service HVAC annually"
- "Clean gutters"
- "Trim trees near house"

**Not Repair Items:** Recommendations for upkeep.

---

## Inspection Process Flow

### Standard Timeline

**In Rider:**
> "You have X amount of days to do your inspections... usually it's 10 days."

**Process:**
1. Inspection period begins (attorney review concluded)
2. Buyer schedules inspections
3. Reports received
4. Request list submitted
5. Seller responds
6. Negotiation if needed
7. Resolution or cancellation

### Response Requirements

**Reports and Requests Due:**
- Within inspection period
- Include all items
- Provide supporting documentation

**Seller Response:**
- Within X days of receiving requests
- Accept, reject, or counter

### If No Agreement

> "If we can't come to an agreement then cancel the contract and we move on."

## How Tony Works Inspections

### Agent-First Approach

> "I let the agents review the report with the clients first and bring me a list."

**His Process:**
1. Agent reviews report with client
2. Client identifies concerns
3. Agent brings list to attorney
4. Attorney reviews report
5. Attorney adds any obvious items
6. Formal request prepared

### Why This Works

**Agent Benefits:**
- Client education opportunity
- Manage expectations
- Filter unreasonable requests

**Attorney Benefits:**
- Focused list to work with
- Client already understands issues
- Less back-and-forth

### Attorney's Role

> "On the back end, once you go over it with the clients and bring me a list, I am going to review the report. If there's something glaringly obvious that's missing or something that I know in routine practice usually gets fixed or we should ask for, I will make those suggestions."

---

## Unreasonable Requests

### What to Avoid

> "If there's something really ridiculous you're asking for, I'm gonna tell you. Unless you want to piss the sellers off, I think you should just go get the gutter extensions at Home Depot for five dollars and put them on yourself."

**Unreasonable Examples:**
- $5 gutter extensions
- Light bulb replacements
- Caulking touch-ups
- Minor cosmetic items

### The Balance

**Fight for:**
- Safety issues
- Material defects
- Significant repairs

**Let go:**
- Minor items
- DIY fixes
- Cosmetic concerns

---

## Additional Testing Requirements

### Suspected Hazardous Materials

> "If there's anything that requires additional testing, so if there's suspected asbestos, we always require the buyer to be responsible to obtain an actual analysis confirming that it's that substance before we agree to anything."

**Common Additional Tests:**
- Asbestos lab analysis
- Mold testing
- Radon measurement
- Lead paint testing

### Why Lab Confirmation Matters

**Home Inspector Says:** "Suspected asbestos"
**Before Agreeing to Remediation:** Get lab confirmation
**Cost vs. Risk:** Lab test ($200-500) vs. unnecessary remediation ($5,000+)

---

## Special Inspections

### Septic Systems

> "Septic or something like that becomes a whole thing."

**What's Involved:**
- Pump and inspect
- Check for failures
- May need certification
- Timeline implications

### Oil Tanks

> "Oil tanks are always removed, even if they're abandoned."

**The Standard:**
- Underground tanks must be removed
- Abandoned tanks included
- Soil sampling if discharge
- Remediation if contamination

### Process If Issues Found

> "If there's discharge for example, we're going to want soil samples. It's going to have to be remediated appropriately, might hold up the transaction."

---

## The Window Example

### Why Small Things Matter

> "A lot of the times even with windows, people think 'oh it's not a big deal, it's just a little mechanism that's not working.' That's a safety hazard. If you can't open a window, if it slams shut, it's a problem."

**Windows as Safety Items:**
- Egress for emergencies
- Injury risk if slams
- Should be included in requests

---

## Negotiation Strategies

### For Buyer's Agents

1. **Prioritize:** Focus on significant items
2. **Document:** Photos and inspector notes
3. **Estimate:** Get repair costs
4. **Be Reasonable:** Drop minor items
5. **Negotiate Credits:** Sometimes better than repairs

### For Listing Agents

1. **Review First:** Understand all requests
2. **Get Estimates:** Know actual costs
3. **Counter Strategically:** Offer credits vs. repairs
4. **Know Limits:** What seller will/won't do
5. **Document Responses:** In writing

### Practical Reality

> "A lot of the times in practical circumstances... if you guys negotiate something and just say 'look, we're not going to go get this, you know, we think it's asbestos, it's probably going to cost $5,000, we're fine with it.' Fine."

**Most Deals:** Parties negotiate a number without extensive testing.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Attorney Rewrites:** Inspection section completely rewritten
2. **Material Defects Standard:** Safety, value, habitability
3. **Standard Exclusions:** Beyond useful life, upgrades (with carve-outs), maintenance
4. **Be Reasonable:** Don't ask for $5 items
5. **Lab Confirmation:** Before major hazardous material claims
6. **Team Approach:** Agent reviews with client first, then attorney
