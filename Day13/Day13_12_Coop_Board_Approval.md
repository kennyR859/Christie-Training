# Day 13, Topic 12: Co-op Board Approval Process

## Overview

The co-op board approval process is unique in real estate and can determine whether a transaction proceeds or fails. This topic covers financial requirements, preparation strategies, and how to help clients successfully navigate board approval.

---

## Why Boards Are Strict

### The Underlying Risk

**What's at Stake:**
- All shareholders share underlying mortgage
- One default affects everyone
- Bank can foreclose on entire building
- Everyone loses if association fails

**Board Perspective:**
> "If they allow people to buy that are not highly qualified, they're at risk of not getting that monthly payment to pay the underlying mortgage."

### New Jersey Law

**Important:**
> "They can only reject an applicant for financial reasons in New Jersey."

**However:**
> "Anybody can look at anybody's finances and always find something that could be a red flag."

### Protect Through Process

**Framing for Clients:**
> "This is a good thing that they're being strict. Once you're in, you don't have to worry about people not making their maintenance payments and everyone losing their property."

---

## Financial Requirements

### Down Payment Standards

**Typical Range:**
- Minimum: 20%
- Common: 25%
- Some boards: 30-35%

**Board Preference:**
Higher down payment = lower risk = better candidate

### Income to Debt Ratio

**Standards:**
- 3:1 ratio (income to debt)
- 4:1 ratio (more conservative boards)

**What This Means:**
```
If monthly debt = $5,000
Income needed at 4:1 = $20,000/month
Annual income needed = $240,000
```

### Rising Income Preference

**What Boards Look For:**
> "Boards prefer applicants with a rising income. They're going to look at tax records and want to make sure income is on the incline, not the decline."

**Tax Return Review:**
- 2-3 years compared
- Year-over-year increase preferred
- Declining income = red flag

### Self-Employed Requirements

**Additional Scrutiny:**
- 3 years tax returns (not just 2)
- Business stability assessment
- Income consistency evaluation

> "Self-employed applicants usually must supply three years of tax returns because they're looking for stability and increase."

---

## Post-Close Liquid Assets

### The Requirement

**What It Means:**
Money remaining AFTER purchase that could cover payments if income lost.

**Accepted Assets:**
- Cash
- Mutual funds
- Liquid investments

**NOT Accepted:**
- Retirement accounts (usually)
- Home equity
- Illiquid investments

### Calculation Formula

```
Monthly Loan Payment + Monthly Maintenance = Total Monthly Obligation

Total Monthly Obligation × 12-24 months = Required Liquid Assets
```

**Example:**
- Loan payment: $3,000
- Maintenance: $1,500
- Total: $4,500/month
- Requirement (12 months): $54,000
- Requirement (24 months): $108,000

### Why It Matters

**Board Logic:**
> "They're looking to see how long the buyer could last making payments if they lost their source of income."

## Preparing the Financial Package

### The Binder System

**Create Organized Presentation:**

**Tab 1: Income Documentation**
- Pay stubs (most recent month)
- W-2s (2-3 years)
- Tax returns (2-3 years)

**Tab 2: Employment Verification**
- Employment letter
- Business documentation (if self-employed)

**Tab 3: Banking**
- Bank statements (3-6 months)
- Savings accounts
- Money market accounts

**Tab 4: Investments**
- Brokerage statements
- Mutual fund statements
- Retirement accounts (for reference)

**Tab 5: Debt Obligations**
- Current rent receipts
- Car payments
- Other loan documentation

**Tab 6: References**
- Professional references
- Landlord references
- Personal references

### Why Organization Matters

> "When you go in front of a board with everything organized and separated, they love it. Some people bring a stack of papers and the board has to wade through them - they hate that. And they're volunteers - they're not getting paid."

---

## The Board Meeting

### Preparation

**Before the Meeting:**
1. Complete financial package
2. Have signed contract
3. Know what to expect
4. Dress professionally
5. Prepare answers to common questions

**Contract Requirement:**
> "Co-op board will not meet with anybody unless they have an agreed-upon contract. It's not an LOI - it's the contract that the attorney actually draws up that both sides sign."

### During the Meeting

**Presentation Tips:**
- Be respectful and low-key
- Don't try too hard to impress
- Answer questions directly
- Let your financials speak

> "When your clients go to that meeting, it behooves them to be very respectful, very low key."

### Social Media Warning

**Boards Will Check:**
> "They're going to check social media. They're going to see because some people do stupid things."

**Example:**
> "They go in front of the co-op board and they're very nice and polite. Then they go online: 'I just went to this co-op board - what a bunch of jerks.' When they read that, they look at the finances and find something to turn you down."

## Rejection Reasons

### New Jersey (Financial Only)

**Legitimate Reasons:**
- Insufficient income
- Too much debt
- Declining income trend
- Inadequate liquid assets
- Credit issues
- Incomplete documentation

### New York (More Expansive)

**Additional Reasons (varies by county):**
- Lifestyle concerns
- References
- Interview performance
- Building fit

> "New York is totally different. Fair housing laws have to do with different counties - different rules in Manhattan than Westchester."

### When Rejection Happens

**Impact:**
- Transaction fails
- Time and money invested lost
- Must start over
- Cannot easily challenge

**Prevention:**
- Pre-qualify buyers thoroughly
- Prepare excellent packages
- Coach on presentation
- Verify all requirements met

## Timeline Considerations

### Standard Process

1. **Offer Accepted** (Day 1)
2. **Attorney Review** (Days 1-4)
3. **Contract Signed** (Week 1-2)
4. **Financial Package Submitted** (Week 2-3)
5. **Board Reviews** (Week 3-5)
6. **Board Meeting Scheduled** (Week 5-7)
7. **Decision Made** (Week 6-8)
8. **Closing** (Week 8-12)

### Variables

**Can Extend Timeline:**
- Incomplete documentation
- Board meeting schedules
- Holiday delays
- Additional questions
- Second interviews (rare)

### Setting Expectations

**For Buyers:**
"Co-op purchases typically take longer than condo purchases because of the board approval process. Plan for 2-3 months from contract to close, possibly longer."

**For Sellers:**
"We need buyers who can pass board approval. I'll help screen for financial qualifications, but we should plan for the timeline board approval requires."

---

## Helping Buyers Who Don't Currently Qualify

### Assessment

**When Buyer Falls Short:**
1. Identify specific shortfalls
2. Calculate what's needed
3. Create improvement plan
4. Set realistic timeline

### Common Solutions

**Insufficient Down Payment:**
- Save more
- Gift from family (verify board accepts)
- Liquidate other assets
- Wait for bonus/windfall

**Income Concerns:**
- Wait for raises
- Document income growth
- Add co-applicant
- Build longer track record

**Liquid Asset Shortfall:**
- Shift retirement to liquid
- Build savings over time
- Consider less expensive unit

### Maintaining Relationship

**Agent's Role:**
> "Even the people that don't qualify, you want to stay in touch with them. Knowing these requirements could actually start changing their habits into creating reserve funds."

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Qualified Buyer, Nervous
Buyer meets all financial requirements but dreads board meeting.

**Coaching:**
"You've done the hard part - your finances are strong. The meeting is just them confirming what's on paper. Be yourself, be respectful, answer directly. Your organized package already impressed them."

### Scenario 2: Income Just Below Requirement
Buyer's income is close but slightly under 4:1 ratio.

**Options:**
1. Target buildings with 3:1 requirement
2. Wait for salary increase
3. Add qualified co-applicant
4. Make larger down payment (reduces loan amount)

### Scenario 3: Social Media Concern
Buyer has complained about landlords online.

**Guidance:**
"Board members often check social media. I'd recommend reviewing your profiles and removing or making private any posts that could be seen negatively. Going forward, avoid posting anything about property dealings."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Financial reasons only (NJ)** - But finances can always be questioned
2. **Organize the package** - Professionalism matters
3. **Rising income preferred** - Show stability and growth
4. **Post-close liquidity crucial** - 12-24 months reserves
5. **Contract required for meeting** - Not just letter of intent
6. **Social media matters** - Boards will check
7. **Respect goes far** - Attitude affects perception
8. **Plan for longer timeline** - Co-ops take more time
