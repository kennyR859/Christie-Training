# Day 13, Topic 3: Attorney Review Process

## Overview

Understanding the attorney review process is essential for New Jersey real estate agents. This topic covers the differences between New Jersey and New York systems, how to find qualified attorneys, and strategies for working with attorneys who may lack experience in specific transaction types.

---

## New Jersey Attorney Review

### Why It Exists

**The Reason:**
- Agents write the purchase contracts in New Jersey
- Agents are not attorneys
- 3-day review period allows legal counsel to review agent-drafted contracts
- Protects all parties from potential errors

### The Process

**Timeline:**
1. Contract signed by both parties
2. 3-day attorney review period begins
3. Either attorney can cancel or modify
4. Extensions can be requested
5. Once approved, contract is binding

**What Attorneys Do:**
- Review all contract terms
- Negotiate language modifications
- Add protective clauses
- Request additional documentation
- Approve or disapprove

### Attorney Review Letters

**Purpose:**
- Document requested changes
- Protect client interests
- Establish negotiating position
- Create paper trail

**When Missing:**
> "I kept waiting to hear from the attorney - waiting for the attorney review letter. I haven't gotten anything, not even an extension."

**What Happened:**
The New York attorney asked the agent to write the letter - demonstrating unfamiliarity with NJ process.

## New Jersey vs New York Systems

### Fundamental Differences

| Aspect | New Jersey | New York |
|--------|------------|----------|
| **Who Writes Contract** | Agent | Attorney |
| **Initial Document** | Purchase Contract | Letter of Intent (LOI) |
| **LOI Enforceability** | Not used | Not enforceable |
| **Attorney Review** | 3 days after contract | No review period |
| **Work Timing** | Done before closing | Much done at closing |
| **Closing Duration** | Usually quick | Can take hours |

### New York Process Explained

**Step by Step:**
1. Agent writes Letter of Intent (LOI)
2. LOI sent to attorneys
3. Home inspection conducted
4. If inspection passes, attorney draws contract
5. Signed contract is binding immediately
6. Closing involves significant work

**Why No Attorney Review:**
- Attorney writes the contract from the start
- No need to review agent's work
- Contract is already legally sound

### Real Experience

**NY Closing Nightmare:**
> "I got into the office in Newark at 3:00 in the afternoon for the closing - we didn't leave till 7:30 at night. They do everything at the closing."

**NJ Difference:**
> "New Jersey attorneys do everything before the closing. It's very ceremonial."

## Finding the Right Attorney

### Essential Requirements

1. **Licensed in Property's State**
   - New Jersey property = NJ licensed attorney
   - Don't accept NY attorney for NJ property

2. **Experience with Property Type**
   - Condo sales require condo experience
   - Co-op sales require co-op experience
   - Different documentation and processes

3. **Dedicated Real Estate Practice**
   - "Real estate attorneys, not attorneys who also do real estate"
   - Labor attorneys, criminal attorneys - not appropriate

### Red Flags

**Watch For:**
- Attorney unfamiliar with CCRs
- Doesn't know to request HOA financials
- Missing board meeting minutes requests
- Asks agent to write attorney review letter
- Suggests inappropriate timeline

**How to Handle:**
> "If your client says 'my brother's an attorney' and he's never handled a condo or co-op sale - educate by asking questions."

---

## Educating Inexperienced Attorneys

### The Question Technique

**Instead of Telling, Ask:**
- "When do you think you'll be getting the CCRs?"
- "Have you requested the HOA financials?"
- "Did you get the board meeting minutes?"

**Why This Works:**
- Non-confrontational
- Attorney learns what they need to do
- You've guided them without insulting
- Creates professional collaboration

### What They Should Request

**For Condos:**
1. CCRs (Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions)
2. HOA financial statements
3. Insurance policies
4. Board meeting minutes (2-3 months)
5. Special assessment information
6. Reserve fund status

**For Co-ops:**
Additional requirements:
- Proprietary lease
- Share certificate details
- Building financial statements
- Board application requirements

## South Jersey Differences

### The Reality

**Geography Matters:**
- North Jersey: Attorneys standard
- Central Jersey: Mixed (50/50)
- South Jersey: Often no attorneys

**Regional Influences:**
- North Jersey influenced by NYC
- South Jersey influenced by Philadelphia
- Different market practices evolved

### When No Attorney Involved

**Agent Responsibility:**
> "Stay in our lane. We negotiate the contract for sale, but once signed, we're advisors and timekeepers."

**Risk Warning:**
> "You've got to be very careful that you don't get caught into negotiating certain things that you're not allowed to negotiate."

### Title Company Role

**In South Jersey:**
- Title companies often handle closings
- May provide some legal functions
- Buyers may think title attorney represents them
- Title company represents the title company

**Disclosure Concern:**
> "A lot of times the buyer thinks that attorney is representing them, and they're not - they represent the title company."

## Advising Clients on Attorneys

### When Clients Refuse Attorney

**Your Obligation:**
1. Strongly advise they hire an attorney
2. Document your advice
3. Have manager also advise
4. Get written acknowledgment

**Sample Advice:**
> "I advise you very strongly to hire an attorney so that you're protected, otherwise there's an imbalance in the negotiating."

### Documentation

**Text Confirmation:**
After verbal advice, send text:
```
[Client Name], as we discussed, I strongly
recommend you hire a real estate attorney
to represent your interests in this transaction.
Please confirm you received this recommendation.
```

**Manager Backup:**
> "I called her manager and asked him to do it too because I can see it now - something happens, she doesn't have an attorney, 'nobody told me I had to have one.'"

## Working with Attorneys

### Communication Best Practices

**Keep Attorneys Informed:**
- Timeline updates
- Inspection results
- Buyer/seller communications
- Issues that arise

**Respect Boundaries:**
- Don't give legal advice
- Forward legal questions to attorney
- Don't negotiate legal terms
- Know when to step back

### When Attorneys Clash

**New Agent Challenge:**
- Unfamiliar attorney may create problems
- Experienced agent works around issues
- Build relationships with good attorneys
- Recommend proven attorneys to clients

**Illegal Practice of Law:**
> "The realtor was trying to negotiate with my attorney. My attorney said 'that's the illegal practice of law - you can't negotiate with me.'"

## Co-op Special Requirements

### Contract vs LOI

**Important Distinction:**
- Co-op board won't meet without signed contract
- LOI not sufficient for board meeting
- Attorney must draft actual contract first
- Both sides must sign before board schedules meeting

**Process:**
1. Write Offer to Purchase (LOI in ZIP forms)
2. Upon acceptance, forward to attorneys
3. Attorneys draft binding contract
4. Both parties sign
5. Then schedule board meeting

### Finding LOI in ZIP Forms

**Note:**
> "If you're looking for Letter of Intent in ZIP forms, it's not called that - it's 'Offer to Purchase.' I looked for days."

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: NY Attorney for NJ Property
Client insists on using their friend, a NY attorney, for NJ condo purchase.

**Response:**
"I understand you trust your friend. However, New Jersey requires specific procedures that a New York attorney may not be familiar with. Would your friend consider partnering with a NJ attorney, or would you like recommendations for experienced NJ condo attorneys?"

### Scenario 2: Attorney Review Never Received
Three days into attorney review, you haven't received any communication.

**Action Steps:**
1. Call both attorneys
2. Request extension if needed
3. Document all contact attempts
4. Inform your client of status
5. Don't let deadline pass silently

### Scenario 3: Client Refusing Attorney in North Jersey
First-time buyer says attorneys are waste of money.

**Response:**
"I strongly recommend you hire an attorney. The contract is a legal document that will obligate you significantly. An experienced real estate attorney typically costs $1,500-2,500 and can protect you from costly mistakes. I'm documenting this conversation and will send you a written confirmation of my recommendation."

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **NJ has attorney review because agents write contracts** - understand why it exists
2. **NY is completely different** - don't assume processes are the same
3. **Attorney experience matters** - condo and co-op require specific knowledge
4. **Educate through questions** - guide inexperienced attorneys diplomatically
5. **South Jersey varies** - know your market's practices
6. **Document attorney recommendations** - protect yourself when clients refuse
7. **Stay in your lane** - know where agent work ends and legal work begins
