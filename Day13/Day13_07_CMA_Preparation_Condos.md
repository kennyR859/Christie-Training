# Day 13, Topic 7: CMA Preparation for Condos

## Overview

The Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) is one of the most important documents a real estate agent produces. For condominiums, there are specific strategies and considerations that differ from single-family homes. This topic covers proper CMA preparation for condo properties.

---

## CMA Fundamentals

### What Is a CMA

**Definition:**
A Comparative Market Analysis compares a subject property to recently sold, currently listed, and expired properties to determine market value.

**Importance:**
> "Probably the most important document you're ever going to have to produce from scratch is a CMA. People want to know the value of their home."

### The Critical Disclaimer

**Value Is Temporal:**
- CMA value is valid ONLY for the day it's prepared
- Markets change constantly
- Conditions can shift quickly

**Client Communication:**
> "Explain to them that this is based on current numbers as of today. Moving forward, things can change."

**Why This Matters:**
- Protects you from complaints
- Sets realistic expectations
- Acknowledges market uncertainty

**The Truth:**
> "We don't have control over the market. If we did, if we had that kind of clairvoyance, I'd be in Atlantic City at the tables."

---

## Finding Comparables for Condos

### Search Priority Order

**1. Same Complex - Same Unit Type:**
- Best comparables are within the building
- Look for similar units sold recently
- Same floor plan = minimal adjustments

**2. The Stack Method:**
- Units directly above or below
- Same layout on different floors
- Only differences are interior finishes

**3. Same Floor Level:**
- Corner units vs middle units
- Similar views and exposure
- Comparable square footage

**4. Square Footage Match:**
- When identical units unavailable
- Similar size units in complex
- Then similar size in nearby complexes

---

## The Stack Method Explained

### What Is the Stack

**Definition:**
In many buildings, units stack vertically with identical layouts floor to floor.

**Example:**
```
Floor 10: Unit 10E - Corner unit, 1,200 sq ft
Floor 9:  Unit 9E  - Corner unit, 1,200 sq ft
Floor 8:  Unit 8E  - Corner unit, 1,200 sq ft
Floor 7:  Unit 7E  - Corner unit, 1,200 sq ft
```

### Why Stack Comparisons Work

**Identical Features:**
- Same square footage
- Same room layout
- Same exposure/direction
- Same building systems

**Only Differences:**
- Interior finishes
- Kitchen updates
- Bathroom renovations
- Flooring choices
- Personal modifications

### Using Stack for CMA

**Process:**
1. Identify subject unit's stack
2. Search for recent sales in same stack
3. Review finishes of comparables
4. Adjust for finish quality differences
5. Minimal other adjustments needed

**Advantage:**
> "You have a better chance of getting apples to apples and not having to make as many adjustments."

## Square Footage Considerations

### Condos vs Houses

**Condo Square Footage - Usually Accurate:**
- Can't expand a condo unit
- What's recorded is what exists
- Only changes if units merged
- Tax records generally reliable

**House Square Footage - Often Inaccurate:**
- Tax records frequently wrong
- Additions may not be recorded
- Permits may not match reality
- Manual measurement often needed

**Instructor's Approach:**
> "When it comes to one to four family houses, I don't go near square footage myself because the tax records are incredibly inaccurate."

### When to Use Square Footage

**For Condos:**
- Good metric when units aren't identical
- Can compare across different complexes
- Calculate price per square foot
- Adjust for building quality

**For Houses:**
- Verify measurements first
- Don't trust tax records
- Consider using appraiser
- Note discrepancies

## Researching the Complex

### Why Research Matters

**Even If You Know the Complex:**
- Things change
- Other agents may know more
- Listings reveal information
- Markets shift

**How to Research:**
1. Review past sales in complex
2. Read other agent listings
3. Check for patterns in pricing
4. Note what sells quickly vs sits

### Management Company Contact

**Information to Request:**
- Current maintenance fees
- Parking fees (indoor vs outdoor)
- What's included in maintenance
- Recent or upcoming assessments
- Rental policy
- Building rules

**Fee Variations:**
> "Some units have to pay for parking separate from maintenance. Indoor parking was $75/month, outdoor was $50/month."

---

## Showing and Access Information

### Building-Specific Questions

**Before Listing or Showing:**
1. Building showing restrictions (times/days)
2. Access procedure (lockbox, concierge, key pickup)
3. Parking for showings
4. Client restrictions (pets, children)
5. Photography permissions

### Access Types

| Access Method | Description | Considerations |
|--------------|-------------|----------------|
| Lockbox on unit | Standard key access | Most convenient |
| Lockbox on rack | Common area location | Must identify yours |
| Concierge/doorman | Key from front desk | Allow extra time |
| Agent office | Pickup at listing office | Most inconvenient |
| Owner present | Owner must be there | Schedule dependent |

### Lockbox Challenges

**Multiple Lockboxes:**
> "There were 15 lockboxes on the bicycle rack. I called the listing agent - 'it's the 3rd one from the right.' It was crazy."

**Solution:**
- Identify your lockbox clearly
- Use colored ribbon or marker
- Can't put unit number on lockbox
- Make it findable for other agents

### Buildings Without Lockboxes

**Examples:**
- East Rutherford River Renaissance - no lockboxes allowed
- Some Fort Lee buildings - concierge only
- Lynwood Village - people use fence across street

**Impact:**
> "If I have to go to the listing agent's office, pick up a key, show the place, then drop the key back - I'm not showing your place unless there's nothing else."

## CMA Presentation Tips

### Setting Expectations

**Frame the Conversation:**
1. Explain CMA is point-in-time value
2. Markets can change
3. Final price is market-determined
4. CMA is starting point, not guarantee

### Condo-Specific Factors

**Include in Analysis:**
- HOA fees (impact affordability)
- Special assessments (past and pending)
- Parking included or extra
- Storage unit value
- Floor level premium/discount
- View premium
- Recent building improvements

### Adjustments for Condos

**Common Adjustments:**
- Floor level (higher often premium)
- View (city, water, park vs parking lot)
- Unit updates (kitchen, bath)
- Parking (deeded vs assigned vs none)
- Storage included
- Balcony/patio

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: No Recent Sales in Complex
Client wants CMA for unit, but no sales in past 6 months in building.

**Approach:**
1. Expand timeframe (look back 12 months)
2. Check stack for any sales
3. Look at similar complexes nearby
4. Use square footage comparison
5. Adjust for building quality differences
6. Note limited data in presentation

### Scenario 2: Assessment Announced
Preparing CMA when special assessment just announced.

**Considerations:**
1. Factor assessment into pricing
2. Recent sales may not reflect new cost
3. Buyers will deduct from offers
4. Be transparent about impact
5. Compare to pre-assessment sales
6. Note in CMA presentation

### Scenario 3: Unique Unit Configuration
Unit has been expanded by buying adjacent unit.

**Approach:**
1. Can't use stack method
2. Find other expanded units
3. Calculate merged square footage
4. Price per square foot comparison
5. Note uniqueness in CMA
6. May need wider geographic search

## Key Takeaways

1. **CMA value is date-specific** - Always communicate this
2. **Stack method is best for condos** - Minimizes adjustments
3. **Condo square footage is reliable** - Unlike houses
4. **Research the complex** - Even if you know it
5. **Contact management company** - Get current fees and rules
6. **Access matters for value** - Lockbox availability affects showings
7. **Use technology** - Platform CRM simplifies process

## Quick Reference

### CMA Comparable Search Order

1. [ ] Same complex, same unit type
2. [ ] Same stack (vertical)
3. [ ] Same floor, similar unit
4. [ ] Same complex, similar square footage
5. [ ] Nearby complex, similar unit
6. [ ] Expand geographic area if needed

### Management Company Questions

- [ ] Current maintenance fee
- [ ] Parking fees (if separate)
- [ ] What's included in maintenance
- [ ] Special assessment history
- [ ] Pending assessments
- [ ] Rental policy
- [ ] Showing restrictions

- [ ] Floor level (+/- per floor)
- [ ] View quality
- [ ] Updates/renovations
- [ ] Parking situation
- [ ] Storage included
- [ ] Balcony/outdoor space
