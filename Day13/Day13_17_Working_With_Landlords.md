# Day 13, Topic 17: Working with Landlords

## Overview

Landlords represent one of the most valuable client types for real estate agents. This topic covers the landlord's responsibilities, how to provide value as an agent, and strategies for building long-term landlord relationships.

---

## Why Landlords Are Valuable

### Repeat Business Model

**Transaction Frequency:**
- Each tenant turnover = commission opportunity
- Average tenancy: 3-5 years
- Multiple properties = multiple opportunities
- Expansion purchases over time

**Example:**
> "I have some landlords been with me for 12 years."

### Business Growth

**From Rentals to Sales:**
- Landlords may buy more properties
- Need agent for purchases
- Need agent for eventual sales
- Long-term relationship value

---

## Landlord Responsibilities

### Required Inspections

**Certificate of Occupancy (CO):**
- Required in some municipalities
- Must be obtained before renting
- Landlord responsibility
- Verify local requirements

**Fire Prevention Inspection:**
- State law requirement
- All 1-4 family rentals
- Must be current
- Landlord pays

**Lead Paint Inspection:**
- Pre-1978 buildings only
- Two types: visual and dust wipe
- Required at each turnover
- Required every 2 years during tenancy
- Landlord pays

### Three+ Family Buildings

**Additional Requirements:**
- Fall under multiple dwelling laws
- Different inspection schedules
- More stringent standards
- Separate regulations

## Your Value Proposition

### 1. Market Analysis

**What You Provide:**
> "We're going to comp their properties and analyze the value."

**Specifically:**
- Current rental market rates
- Competition analysis
- Pricing recommendations
- Market timing advice

**For Problematic Listings:**
Help identify why property isn't renting:
- Overpriced?
- Better competing options?
- Condition issues?

### 2. Legal Knowledge

**Critical Value:**
> "We can explain all the changes in the laws - especially in the last two years."

**Areas of Expertise:**
- Lead paint inspection changes
- Fair housing requirements
- Lease requirements
- Disclosure obligations
- Inspection standards

**Why This Matters:**
> "Changes in the laws put landlords at risk if they don't know the law. We stay on top of changes regularly."

### 3. Marketing and Advertising

**Your Services:**
> "Landlord doesn't have to worry about advertising."

**What You Handle:**
- MLS listing
- Professional photography
- Online syndication
- Social media marketing
- Signage (if applicable)

### 4. Tenant Screening

**Quality Assurance:**
> "We're going to have more qualified tenants because we're running credit reports and background checks."

**Screening Components:**
- Credit check
- Background check
- Income verification
- Rental history
- Employment verification
- References

### 5. Showing Management

**Time Savings:**
> "We will schedule the showings. The landlord doesn't have to be there or be inconvenienced."

**What You Handle:**
- Coordinate schedules
- Access management
- Professional presentation
- Follow-up with prospects

### 6. Interview Facilitation

**Added Service:**
> "We will offer an interview."

**Benefits:**
- Pre-screen candidates
- Assess tenant quality
- Present best options
- Reduce landlord's work

## Building Landlord Relationships

### Initial Contact

**Approach:**
- Professional introduction
- Value proposition clear
- No pressure
- Long-term focus

**Script:**
"I noticed you have rental properties in [area]. I specialize in helping landlords minimize vacancy and find qualified tenants. I'd love to introduce myself in case you ever need assistance with your rentals."

### Providing Value First

**Before Business:**
- Share market information
- Provide legal updates
- Answer questions freely
- Be a resource

### Staying Connected

**Regular Touch Points:**
- Market updates
- Legal changes
- Relevant news
- Check-ins

## Tenant-Landlord Interactions

### The Different Dynamic

**Unlike Buyer-Seller:**
> "I want to keep buyers and sellers away from each other because they can compromise their negotiating position."

**For Tenant-Landlord:**
> "Tenants and landlords - I love when they talk to each other."

### Why Interaction Helps

**Benefits:**
- Landlord sees tenant quality
- Tenant asks questions directly
- Relationship begins early
- Reduces later issues

**Example:**
> "I love when I show a unit and the landlord's there. They see my people, get a feeling for them, answer questions right on the spot. They're already building a relationship."

### Interview Benefits

**When Done Well:**
> "I've done interviews where people should have been turned down because they had a pet, but after the interview, the landlord liked them and liked the pet."

## Problematic Situations

### Uncooperative Tenants

**The Challenge:**
Current tenants making showings difficult.

**Solutions:**
1. Build relationship with tenant
2. Work around their schedule
3. Offer incentives through landlord

**Incentive Example:**
> "Take $10-15 off rent every time they allow a showing, save business cards, confirm with me."

### Tenants Sabotaging

**The Risk:**
> "Be there FIRST. If the customer gets there first and talks to the tenant, the tenant might say 'this is a horrible house, the basement leaks.'"

**Prevention:**
- Always arrive before prospects
- Build tenant relationship
- Address concerns proactively
- Have landlord communicate expectations

### Long Vacancies

**When Properties Don't Rent:**
- Analyze pricing
- Assess competition
- Review condition
- Check showing feedback
- Adjust strategy

## Expanding the Relationship

### From Rentals to Sales

**Natural Progression:**
- Landlord trusts you
- Needs to buy property
- Calls you for purchase
- Relationship deepens

**Example:**

### Multiple Properties

**Growth Pattern:**
- Start with one property
- Successful experience
- Add more properties
- Become preferred agent

### Property Sales

**Eventually:**
- Landlords sell properties
- You're trusted advisor
- Natural listing opportunity
- Full-service relationship

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: New Landlord Prospecting
You identified a property owner who self-manages a rental.

**Approach:**
1. Research the property
2. Prepare value proposition
3. Contact professionally
4. Focus on solving problems
5. Offer to be a resource

### Scenario 2: Problem Tenant During Showing
Current tenant is making showings difficult.

**Solution:**
1. Meet tenant one-on-one
2. Understand their concerns
3. Discuss with landlord about incentives
4. Create win-win solution
5. Document agreements

### Scenario 3: Long-Term Relationship Building
Landlord just used you for first rental.

**Follow-Up Plan:**
1. Thank you after lease signed
2. Check in at 30 days
3. Provide market updates quarterly
4. Call on tenant's anniversary
5. Be available for questions
6. Ask about future properties

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Landlords = repeat business** - Most valuable client type
2. **Know legal requirements** - Your expertise adds value
3. **Let tenants and landlords meet** - Builds relationships
4. **Fee often paid by tenant** - No cost to landlord
5. **Handle difficult tenants** - Part of your value
6. **Think long-term** - One landlord can mean years of business
7. **Be a resource first** - Business follows trust
