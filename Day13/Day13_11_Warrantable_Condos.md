# Day 13, Topic 11: Warrantable vs Non-Warrantable Condos

## Overview

Understanding warrantable and non-warrantable condo classifications is essential for advising buyers on financing options. This topic covers what makes a condo warrantable, common disqualifiers, and alternative financing solutions for non-warrantable properties.

---

## What Is a Warrantable Condo?

### Definition

**Warrantable = Approved for conventional financing by:**
- Fannie Mae
- Freddie Mac
- FHA (Federal Housing Administration)
- VA (Veterans Affairs)

### Why Warrantability Matters

**For Buyers:**
- Standard mortgage available
- Better interest rates
- Lower down payments possible
- More lender options

**For Sellers:**
- Larger buyer pool
- Faster sales
- Easier closings
- Better resale value

---

## Fannie Mae Guidelines

### Key Requirements

**For Condo to Be Warrantable:**
1. No single owner controls >10% of units
2. Project is complete
3. HOA controls (not developer)
4. Acceptable rental percentage
5. No active litigation against HOA
6. Adequate insurance
7. Sufficient reserve funds
8. No short-term rentals (Airbnb restrictions)

### The Approval Process

**How It Works:**
1. Lender submits condo to Fannie Mae
2. Fannie Mae reviews HOA documents
3. Approval or denial issued
4. Approval valid for specific time period
5. Must be renewed

---

## Non-Warrantable Reasons

### 1. Single Owner Concentration

**The Rule:**
No single person or entity can own more than 10% of total units.

**Real Example:**
> "When I was new, I had a client buying in Garfield - 10 unit complex. The bank said no good. Why? One owner owned four of the 10 units. That made it non-warrantable."

**Impact:**
- Common in small buildings
- Investor-heavy complexes affected
- Developer holdback during sales period

### 2. Project Not Complete

**Common Scenarios:**
- New construction not finished
- Phases still being built
- Common areas incomplete

**Solution:**
> "Most new construction projects - the developer will have a relationship with a preferred lender. Probably the only way you're getting a loan is through that preferred lender."

**Trade-off:**
May pay slightly higher interest rate.

### 3. Developer Controls HOA

**The Issue:**
- New developments start with developer control
- Must hand over to homeowners at certain point
- During developer control = non-warrantable

**Timeline:**
Typically hands over when certain percentage sold.

### 4. High Renter Percentage

**Lender Concern:**
> "A high percentage of units occupied by renters - that's always the biggest thing that turns lenders off."

**Why Lenders Worry:**
- Perceived as high risk
- Higher turnover
- Transient behavior
- Owners may struggle if tenants default

### 5. Short-Term Rentals

**The Problem:**
- Airbnb-friendly buildings
- Vacation rental policies
- Daily/weekly rentals allowed

**Lender View:**
Increased risk from transient occupancy.

### 6. Active Litigation

**Examples:**
- Construction defect lawsuits
- HOA vs developer
- Slip and fall claims
- Insurance disputes

**Impact:**
Uncertainty about financial exposure.

### 7. Insufficient Insurance

**Requirements:**
- Adequate liability coverage
- Property coverage
- Flood insurance (if applicable)
- Fidelity bond

### 8. Inadequate Reserves

**New Requirements:**
Post-Champlain Towers legislation increased reserve requirements.

## Portfolio Loans - The Solution

### What Is a Portfolio Loan?

**Definition:**
A loan where the bank keeps the mortgage in their own portfolio rather than selling it to secondary market.

**Key Differences:**
| Standard Loan | Portfolio Loan |
|--------------|----------------|
| Sold to Fannie/Freddie | Kept by lender |
| Must meet guidelines | More flexible |
| Standard rates | Higher rates |
| Standard down payment | Higher down payment |

### How Banks Make Money

**Standard Process:**
1. Bank originates loan
2. Charges fees
3. Sells loan to investors
4. Gets capital back
5. Makes new loans

**Portfolio Process:**
1. Bank originates loan
2. Charges fees
3. Keeps loan on books
4. Earns interest over time
5. More risk = more return needed

### Portfolio Loan Terms

**Typical Requirements:**
- 20-25% down payment (vs 3.5% FHA)
- Higher interest rate (1-2% above standard)
- Strong credit required
- More documentation

**Unity Bank:**
> "Portfolio loans are what Unity Bank specializes in. If somebody really wants in that particular complex that isn't approved, portfolio loan is the answer."

---

## FHA Buyer Limitations

### The Challenge

**FHA Down Payment:**
- Standard: 3.5% down

**Portfolio Loan Down Payment:**
- Typically: 20-25% down

**Result:**
> "FHA buyer - it's not going to work because of the down payment."

### Options for FHA Buyers

1. **Find warrantable condo**
2. **Save larger down payment**
3. **Look at different property types**
4. **Wait for building to become warrantable**

## Researching Before Showing

### How to Check Warrantability

**Method 1: Call Management Company**
- Ask what banks have financed units
- Request list of approved lenders
- Inquire about FHA/VA approval

**Method 2: Check Recent Sales**
- Look at financing type in MLS
- Realist data may show lender
- Tax records sometimes include

**Method 3: Ask Listing Agent**
- Should know financing status
- Can provide lender contacts
- May have recent approval

### Questions to Ask

1. "Is this complex Fannie Mae approved?"
2. "Have FHA or VA loans been done here?"
3. "What banks have financed purchases recently?"
4. "Are there any known warrantability issues?"

## Price Impact

### Does Non-Warrantable Affect Price?

**Theory:**
Lower buyer pool = lower price

**Reality:**
> "It should be lower, but it's not always. Prices are usually determined by supply and demand."

**Factors:**
- Desirable location may overcome
- Cash buyers unaffected
- Portfolio financing available
- Market conditions vary

### Today's Market Reality

**Cash and Gap Financing:**
> "A major number of our buyers are waiving appraisal contingencies. They're making up the difference in cash."

**Example:**
- Sale price: $700,000
- Appraises at: $650,000
- Loan amount: $520,000 (80% of appraisal)
- Buyer brings: $180,000 cash (not $140,000)

---

## Market Outlook

### Current Equity Situation

**Why Condos Are Protected:**
- Owners have significant equity
- Higher down payments common
- Cash invested protects value
- Unlike 2008 crisis

**2008 Comparison:**
> "100% financing back then - zero equity. People could just walk away. Now people are adding cash, big down payments. If values drop, there's so much equity they won't walk."

### Future Considerations

**If Market Declines:**
- Owners will lower prices to sell
- Won't have foreclosure crisis
- Resale possible, not walking away
- Portfolio loan buyers protected by equity

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: First-Time Buyer with FHA
Client pre-approved for FHA loan wants specific condo.

**Discovery:**
Check if condo is FHA approved. If not:
"This complex isn't currently FHA approved. You'd need conventional financing or a portfolio loan requiring 20-25% down. Would you like me to find FHA-approved complexes, or are you able to increase your down payment?"

### Scenario 2: Small Building
Client likes unit in 10-unit building where one investor owns 3 units.

**Explanation:**
"With one owner controlling 30% of the units, this building isn't warrantable for conventional financing. You'd need a portfolio loan with higher down payment and rate. Let me connect you with Unity Bank to discuss options."

### Scenario 3: New Construction
Buyer wants unit in building that's 60% sold.

**Guidance:**
"The developer still controls the HOA and the project isn't complete. You'd likely need to use their preferred lender. Let me find out the terms they're offering and compare to what you'd get elsewhere."

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Warrantable = standard financing available** - Easier for buyers
2. **Non-warrantable isn't impossible** - Just different financing
3. **Portfolio loans are the alternative** - Higher cost, more requirements
4. **FHA buyers need warrantable** - 3.5% down won't work otherwise
5. **Research before showing** - Know financing situation upfront
6. **Small buildings are risky** - Easier to become non-warrantable
7. **Market affects pricing impact** - Supply/demand still rules

## Quick Reference

- [ ] Single owner >10% of units
- [ ] New construction not complete
- [ ] Developer controls HOA
- [ ] High rental percentage

- [ ] Active litigation
- [ ] Inadequate insurance

- [ ] 20-25% down payment available
- [ ] Strong credit score
- [ ] Stable income documented
- [ ] Understand higher rate (1-2% more)
- [ ] Lender identified (Unity Bank)
- [ ] Timeline allows for process

- [ ] Call management company
- [ ] Check recent financing types
- [ ] Ask listing agent
- [ ] Verify with buyer's lender
- [ ] Confirm current status (can change)
