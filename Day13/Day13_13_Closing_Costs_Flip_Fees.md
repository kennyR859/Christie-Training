# Day 13, Topic 13: Closing Costs & Flip Fees

## Overview

Understanding closing costs for condo and co-op transactions helps agents properly advise buyers and sellers on the true cost of their transactions. This topic covers standard closing costs, PMI, mansion tax, and the unique flip fees found in co-op transactions.

---

## Standard Closing Costs

### General Range

**Typical Amount:**
- 2.5% to 5% of purchase price
- Can exceed for new construction
- Varies by transaction complexity

### Components

**Buyer Closing Costs:**
| Item | Typical Cost |
|------|-------------|
| Attorney fees | $1,500-3,000 |
| Title insurance | 0.5-1% of price |
| Recording fees | $200-500 |
| Appraisal | $400-600 |
| Home inspection | $400-600 |
| Credit report | $50-100 |
| Bank fees | Varies |
| Prepaid taxes | Varies |
| Prepaid insurance | Varies |

**Seller Closing Costs:**
| Item | Typical Cost |
|------|-------------|
| Attorney fees | $1,500-3,000 |
| Real estate commission | 5-6% (negotiable) |
| Transfer tax | Varies by state |
| Mansion tax (if applicable) | Varies |
| Flip fee (co-op only) | Varies |
| Outstanding HOA | As owed |
| Prorated taxes | As applicable |

## Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI)

### When Required

**Trigger:**
Down payment less than 20%

### Cost Range

**Annual Rate:**
0.3% to 1.5% of loan amount

**Factors Affecting Rate:**
- Credit score
- Down payment percentage
- Loan type
- Lender policies

### Example Calculation

```
Home Price: $500,000
Down Payment: 10% ($50,000)
Loan Amount: $450,000
PMI Rate: 0.5%

Annual PMI: $450,000 × 0.005 = $2,250
Monthly PMI: $2,250 ÷ 12 = $187.50
```

### Removing PMI

**Standard Path:**
- Pay down to 80% loan-to-value
- Request removal
- May need new appraisal

**Automatic Removal:**
- When loan reaches 78% LTV
- Based on original amortization schedule

## Cash Reserve Requirements

### Standard Requirements

**Minimum:**
12 months of payments

**What "Payments" Includes:**
- Mortgage payment
- Property taxes
- Insurance
- HOA/maintenance fees

### Co-op Additional Requirements

**Typically:**
12-24 months or more

**Why Higher:**
- Board wants financial security
- Underlying mortgage risk
- Building stability concern

---

## Mansion Tax

### What It Is

**Definition:**
Additional tax on high-value property sales

### New Jersey Structure

**Threshold:**
Starts at $1,000,000

**Who Pays:**
> "That's on the seller now - it's not on the buyer."

### Planning Impact

**For Sellers:**
- Factor into net proceeds calculation
- Consider pricing strategies
- Understand tiered rates

**For Buyers:**
- Not typically buyer responsibility
- Verify in contract

## Co-op Flip Fees

### What Is a Flip Fee

**Definition:**
Percentage of sale price charged by co-op association to sellers at time of sale.

**Where It Goes:**
Into association's reserve/capital fund

### Purpose

**Why They Exist:**
1. **Discourage Turnover:**
   > "Co-ops want to keep the same people there. They don't want transients - people coming and going."

2. **Build Reserves:**
   Adds to capital fund for building maintenance

3. **Create Stability:**
   Owners less likely to sell with fee attached

4. **Offset Assessments:**
   Helps fund major repairs without special assessments

### Typical Rates

**Range:**
1% to 6% of sale price

**Manhattan Example:**
> "6% in Manhattan"

### Calculation Example

```
Sale Price: $800,000
Flip Fee: 3%

Flip Fee Amount: $800,000 × 0.03 = $24,000
```

### Impact on Net Proceeds

**Example:**
```
Sale Price: $800,000
Commission (5%): $40,000
Flip Fee (3%): $24,000
Attorney: $2,000
Other costs: $2,000

Total Deductions: $68,000
Net Proceeds: $732,000
```

## Closing Cost Comparison

### Condo vs Co-op Seller

| Cost Item | Condo | Co-op |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Commission | Yes | Yes |
| Attorney | Yes | Yes |
| Transfer Tax | Yes | Yes |
| Mansion Tax | If applicable | If applicable |
| Flip Fee | No | Yes |
| Capital Assessment | Sometimes | Through flip fee |

### New Construction Additional

**Extra Costs Possible:**
- Developer transfer fees
- First-year reserves
- Working capital fund contribution
- Special utility setup fees

**Why Higher:**
> "It could exceed 5% - rare but it could. It's usually new construction where there's more fees."

## Advising Clients

### For Buyers

**Pre-Purchase Discussion:**
1. Estimate total closing costs
2. Factor in PMI if applicable
3. Calculate total cash needed
4. Verify lender requirements

**Cash Needed Calculation:**
```
Down payment
+ Closing costs (estimate 3%)
+ Reserves required
+ Moving costs
+ Initial repairs/improvements
= Total cash needed
```

### For Sellers

**Pre-Listing Discussion:**
1. Calculate expected net proceeds
2. Include all known costs
3. Factor flip fee if co-op
4. Discuss mansion tax if applicable

**Net Proceeds Worksheet:**
```
Expected sale price
- Commission
- Flip fee (co-op)
- Mansion tax (if applicable)
- Attorney fees
- Outstanding HOA
- Transfer taxes
- Mortgage payoff
= Expected net proceeds
```

## Due Diligence Items

### What Attorneys Should Request

**For Both Types:**
- CCRs (Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions)
- HOA/Board financial statements
- Insurance policies
- Board meeting minutes (2-3 months)

**Why Meeting Minutes Matter:**

> "You want to see what they're talking about. Do they have a potential assessment moving forward? Is there a lawsuit coming?"

**Example from Training:**
> "We had a security guard altercation at our building. The discussions at our meetings were about whether to sue the homeowner. All of this could have been in the minutes - potential lawsuits, investigations."

### Insurance Verification

**Critical Items:**
- Adequate liability coverage
- Property coverage for building
- Flood insurance (if required)
- Fidelity bond

**Why Lenders Care:**
> "The bank will also look at insurance policies to make sure there's enough money if they lose a lawsuit."

## Closing Process

### Standard Steps

1. **Document Review:**
   - Attorney reviews all closing documents
   - Inspections completed

2. **Insurance:**
   - Obtain required policies
   - Provide to lender

3. **Final Walkthrough:**
   - Verify condition
   - Check for issues

4. **Closing:**
   - Sign documents
   - Transfer funds
   - Record deed/transfer shares

### Post-Closing

**Photography Permission:**
> "At the end, if you're going to take pictures with your client - get permission. Then if they give verbal permission, send email: 'thanks for approving use of your picture for marketing.'"

**Testimonial Request:**
> "That's the perfect time at closing when everybody's happy to ask for a testimonial."

**ChatGPT Assist:**
> "If they're too busy, ask ChatGPT to write one. Read it to them: 'Is that OK? Can I use your initials?'"

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Buyer Cash Calculation
First-time buyer purchasing $450,000 condo with 10% down.

**Calculation:**
```
Down payment: $45,000
PMI: Required (under 20%)
Closing costs (3%): $13,500
Reserves (3 months): $7,500
Total cash needed: ~$66,000
```

### Scenario 2: Co-op Seller Net Proceeds
Seller in Manhattan co-op, $1.2M sale price, 5% flip fee.

**Calculation:**
```
Sale price: $1,200,000
Commission (5%): $60,000
Flip fee (5%): $60,000
Mansion tax: Yes (over $1M)
Attorney: $3,000
Other: $2,000
Gross deductions: $125,000+
Mortgage payoff: Variable
```

### Scenario 3: Condo vs Co-op Comparison
Buyer considering similar units - one condo, one co-op.

**Explain:**
"The co-op may have lower purchase price, but when you sell someday, you'll pay a flip fee that condos don't have. Also, the down payment requirement is higher for co-ops. Let's calculate total costs both ways."

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Closing costs range 2.5-5%** - Plan accordingly
2. **PMI adds monthly cost** - When under 20% down
3. **Mansion tax hits sellers** - Over $1M in NJ
4. **Flip fees are unique to co-ops** - Factor in seller calculations
5. **Reserves required** - 12-24 months for co-ops
6. **Request board minutes** - Reveals upcoming costs
7. **Get testimonials at closing** - When clients are happiest
