# Day 13, Topic 9: Working with Condo/Co-op Buyers

## Overview

Working with condo and co-op buyers requires thorough discovery and preparation. This topic covers how to identify buyer needs, prepare clients for the purchase process, and set appropriate expectations for different property types.

---

## Initial Consultation

### Understanding Lifestyle Needs

**Housing Type Preferences:**
- High-rise building
- Garden apartments
- Townhouse-style condos
- Gated community
- Adult community (55+)
- Planned Unit Development (PUD)

**Cannot Tell by Looking:**
> "You can't walk in front of a building and say 'that's a condominium' or 'that's a co-op.' You have to find out - do some investigation."

### Location Preferences

**Environment Questions:**
1. **Urban:** Close to services, shopping, entertainment
2. **Suburban:** More space, family-friendly, yards
3. **Rural:** No sidewalks, septic systems, more land

**Distance Considerations:**
- Distance from work
- Distance from family (closer OR farther!)
- Recreation access (golf, beaches, parks)
- School districts
- Transportation/commute

**Real Talk:**
> "Sometimes people want to be closer to their family and sometimes they want to be a little farther away. I had a client say 'when you help my sisters find a place, please make sure they're far away from me - I can't stand my brother-in-law.'"

---

## The Three Needs and Three Wants

### The Question

**Always Ask:**
1. "What are your 3 most important NEEDS?"
2. "What are your 3 most important WANTS?"

### The Difference

**Needs = Baseline Requirements:**
- Must have for property to work
- Non-negotiable items
- Determines minimum property parameters
- Price baseline

**Wants = Nice to Have:**
- Would prefer but can compromise
- Adds to property appeal
- Raises price when present
- Can live without

### Why This Matters

**Finding Dominant Buying Motive:**
> "By knowing the difference from needs and wants, you'll know their dominant buying motive. You can save months of work."

**Avoiding the Trap:**
> "I had clients - after showing three times, I found out they were looking for two different houses. Husband wanted finished basement and detached garage. Wife wanted updated kitchen. They had to have a conversation and come back to me with something I could work with."

---

## Financial Pre-Qualification

### Fair Housing Compliance

**The Rule:**
Ask the SAME questions to EVERY client.

**Why Consistency Matters:**
> "If you ask some people that question and you don't ask everybody, you violate fair housing. There's no degrees with fair housing - if it occurs, you're guilty."

**About Testers:**
> "You don't know who's a tester. You may work with somebody for two months showing them places, then they disappear. They were a tester."

### Pre-Approval vs Pre-Qualification

| Pre-Approval | Pre-Qualification |
|--------------|-------------------|
| Documents submitted | Verbal information |
| Income verified | Income stated |
| Credit pulled | Credit unknown |
| More reliable | Less reliable |
| Stronger offer | Weaker offer |

**Recommendation:**
> "Pre-approval is much better than pre-qualified because they actually have to supply documents."

## Preparing Co-op Buyers

### Financial Package Organization

**Create a Binder:**
- Labeled tabs for each document type
- Professional presentation
- Easy for board to review

**Sections:**
1. Pay stubs
2. Rent receipts
3. Bank statements
4. Investment statements
5. Tax returns
6. Employment verification

**Why Organization Matters:**
> "When you go in front of a board with everything organized and separated, they love it. Some people bring a stack of papers and the board has to wade through them - they hate that. Board members are volunteers - they're not getting paid."

### Setting Expectations

**Explain the Process:**
1. Offer/contract signed
2. Financial package prepared
3. Board reviews documents
4. Board meeting scheduled
5. In-person presentation
6. Board decision

**The Goal:**
> "You want to do everything to help your client win. It's either win or lose, and winning is about proper preparation."

## Finding the Right Attorney

### Essential Requirements

**Must Have:**
1. Licensed in property's state (NJ for NJ property)
2. Experience with property type
3. Dedicated real estate practice

**Red Flags:**
- "Also does real estate"
- Never handled condo/co-op
- NY attorney for NJ property
- General practice lawyer

### Educating Clients

**When Client Suggests Inexperienced Attorney:**
"I understand you trust your friend/family member. However, condo/co-op transactions have specific requirements. Would they consider partnering with an experienced real estate attorney, or would you like some recommendations?"

## Condo-Specific Buyer Needs

### Standard Considerations

**Bedrooms and Bathrooms:**
- Current needs
- Future needs (growing family?)
- Guest accommodations
- Home office requirements

### Condo Amenities

**Common Wants:**
- Updated kitchen and bathrooms
- Business center
- Community room
- Concierge service
- 24-hour security
- Pool
- Theater room
- On-site shopping
- Fitness center
- Spa services

**Senior Preferences:**
> "Seniors often want services in the building - spa, barber, dining. They don't want to leave the building often."

### Floor Level Preferences

**Higher Floors:**
- Better views
- Less street noise
- More privacy
- Generally premium price

**Lower Floors:**
- Easier access
- Less elevator wait
- Quicker exit in emergency
- Seniors may prefer

> "Sometimes seniors want to be on lower floors."

## Parking Considerations

### Questions to Ask

1. How many spaces needed?
2. Indoor or outdoor acceptable?
3. Budget for parking if separate cost?
4. Willing to wait for space?

### Parking Types

**Included vs Extra:**
- Some complexes include parking
- Others charge separately
- Indoor typically costs more

**Hudson County Example:**
> "Having a private garage can add anywhere from $50,000 to $100,000 of value to the condo."

### Deeded vs Assigned

**Deeded Parking:**
- You own the space
- Part of your deed
- Cannot be taken away
- More valuable

**Assigned Parking:**
- Association assigns you a space
- Could potentially be reassigned
- Less security of tenure
- Electric vehicle push may affect assigned spaces

**Future Concern:**
> "With the push for electric vehicles, they're putting in charging stations that reduce parking spaces. If you don't have deeded space, they could take your assigned space away."

---

## Storage and Laundry

### Storage Questions

- Is storage unit included?
- Location of storage (same floor, basement?)
- Size adequate?
- Security of storage area?
- Additional storage available?

### Laundry Options

- In-unit washer/dryer?
- On same floor?
- Building laundry room?
- Quality and availability?

---

## Transportation Needs

### Mass Transit

**Questions:**
- Which train line needed?
- Bus routes nearby?
- Shuttle service available?
- Distance to station?

**Hudson County:**
> "So many people from New Jersey work in New York City. Transportation is really a big influence on housing."

### Commute Considerations

- Drive time to work
- Parking at work
- Public transit feasibility
- Radius from workplace acceptable

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: First Consultation
New buyer wants to look at condos in Bergen County.

**Discovery Process:**
1. "What brings you to condo living?"
2. "What are your 3 must-haves?"
3. "What 3 things would be nice to have?"
4. "Have you been pre-approved?"
5. "What's your timeline?"
6. "Any specific areas you prefer?"

### Scenario 2: Couple with Different Priorities
After several showings, couple seems unable to agree.

**Approach:**
"I'm sensing you might have different priorities. Let's sit down together and each share your top 3 needs and wants. Then we can find where you overlap and where we might need to compromise."

### Scenario 3: Co-op First-Timer
Buyer has never purchased co-op, doesn't understand process.

**Education:**
"A co-op is different from a condo. You're buying shares in a corporation, not real estate. The board must approve you, which means preparing a financial package and meeting with them. It's more involved, but once you're in, this process protects your investment too."

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Ask the same questions to everyone** - Fair housing compliance
2. **Needs vs wants is critical** - Identifies dominant buying motive
3. **Pre-approval beats pre-qualification** - Especially for co-ops
4. **Organize financial packages** - Boards appreciate professionalism
5. **Understand parking implications** - Deeded vs assigned matters
6. **Know transportation needs** - Major factor for commuters
7. **Cultural considerations exist** - Direction, location preferences
