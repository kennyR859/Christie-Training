# Day 13, Topic 18: Rental Inspections & Lead Paint Laws

## Overview

New Jersey rental properties face specific inspection requirements that agents must understand to properly advise landlords. This topic covers fire prevention inspections, lead paint testing requirements, and how to navigate the compliance landscape.

---

## Fire Prevention Inspection

### State Law Requirement

**What It Is:**
All 1-4 family rental properties must have fire prevention inspection.

**Scope:**
- Smoke detectors
- Carbon monoxide detectors
- Fire extinguishers
- Egress windows
- General fire safety

**Who Pays:**
Landlord responsibility

### Timing Requirements

**When Needed:**
- Before tenant moves in
- Periodic renewals (varies by municipality)
- After significant changes to property

### Obtaining Inspection

**Process:**
1. Contact local fire department or designated inspector
2. Schedule inspection
3. Ensure property ready
4. Receive certificate
5. Provide to tenant

## Lead Paint Inspection Requirements

### Why Lead Paint Matters

**Health Risks:**
- Dangerous for children 6 and under
- Attacks immune system
- Attacks organs
- Affects mental capacity
- Children learn by putting things in mouth

**Historical Context:**
> "Lead was a drying agent - it made paint dry faster. It was banned from paint in 1978."

### Properties Affected

**Applicability:**
- Buildings built BEFORE 1978
- Rental properties
- At each tenant turnover
- Every 2 years during tenancy

**Not Applicable:**
- Post-1978 construction
- Owner-occupied homes (different rules)
- Commercial properties (different rules)

---

## Two Types of Inspections

### Visual Inspection

**What It Is:**
Walk-through assessment looking for visible lead paint hazards.

**What Inspector Looks For:**
- Nail holes
- Cracks in walls
- Paint chips
- Peeling paint
- Deteriorated surfaces

**Duration:**
> "The guy was in the apartment maybe three minutes."

**Cost:**
~$100-150 per unit

### Dust Wipe Inspection

**What It Is:**
Physical samples taken and sent to laboratory for analysis.

**Process:**
1. Inspector takes samples from surfaces
2. Samples sent to certified lab
3. Lab analyzes for lead levels
4. Results reported

**Cost:**
~$275+ per unit

**When Required:**
In municipalities with high lead levels in children

---

## How Requirements Are Determined

### State Data Collection

**The System:**
> "The state has been collecting information from the medical world on children six years and younger."

**Process:**
1. Children's blood tested for lead (now mandatory)
2. Results tracked by municipality
3. Towns with high levels = stricter requirements
4. Towns with low levels = basic requirements

### Municipal Determination

**High Lead Levels in Children:**
- Dust wipe test required
- More expensive
- Lab analysis needed

**Low Lead Levels in Children:**
- Visual inspection acceptable
- Less expensive
- Quicker process

### Surprising Results

**Example:**
> "Alpine, NJ - probably the highest zip code in the state, definitely highest in Bergen County - they have to do dust wipe."

**Key Insight:**
> "It has nothing to do with demographics. It has to do with older homes."

---

## Compliance Timing

### When Inspections Required

**At Tenant Turnover:**
- New tenant moving in
- Inspection must be current
- Certificate provided to tenant

**During Tenancy:**
- Every 2 years
- Even with same tenant
- Landlord's responsibility to track

### Conflict with Federal Law

**The Issue:**
> "It conflicts with federal law. Federal law says tenant has opportunity within 10 days to conduct risk assessment at their expense. New Jersey says no - landlord pays and it's required every turnover."

---

## Lead-Free Certification

### What It Is

**Permanent Solution:**
Property certified as lead-free after complete remediation.

**Process:**
1. Complete gut renovation
2. Remove all lead-containing materials
3. Professional certification
4. One-time process

### Costs

**Certification Process:**
~$2,000

**When Worth It:**
> "If you did a total remodel and got rid of all the lead, you can get certified."

**Example:**
> "That particular apartment had been gutted. Yes, because it's gutted, you could get that and never have to get it done again."

### Long-Term Benefit

**Calculation:**
```
Annual inspection cost: $275
Over 10 years: $2,750

One-time certification: $2,000
Savings after ~7 years
Plus: No ongoing hassle
```

## Municipal Variations

### Always Check Current Requirements

**Warning:**
> "You always have to check current because things change."

**How Rules Change:**
- Town council passes ordinance
- State updates requirements
- Enforcement priorities shift

**Best Practice:**
> "If with any of these inspections - always verify with the municipality what's currently required."

### Research Process

1. Contact municipal clerk
2. Check town website
3. Call health department
4. Verify before advising landlord

## Multiple Dwelling Requirements

### Three+ Family Buildings

**Different Rules:**
> "Three family and over - they fall under multiple dwelling laws."

**Additional Requirements:**
- Different inspection schedules
- More stringent standards
- Additional documentation
- Separate regulatory framework

### Why Different

**Higher Risk:**
- More tenants affected
- Greater liability
- More complex systems
- Additional safety concerns

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: New Landlord Questions
First-time landlord asks what inspections they need.

**Response:**
"For rental properties, you'll need a fire prevention inspection - that's state law. If your building was built before 1978, you'll also need lead paint inspection. Let me check what type your municipality requires and get you the current costs."

### Scenario 2: Inspection Expired Mid-Tenancy
Landlord's lead inspection expires with same tenant in place.

**Guidance:**
"Even with the same tenant, lead inspection is required every two years. You'll need to schedule the inspection before the expiration. Would you like me to connect you with an inspector?"

### Scenario 3: Old Building, Major Renovation
Landlord completely gutted pre-1978 building.

**Advice:**
"Since you did a complete gut renovation, you might qualify for lead-free certification. It costs about $2,000 one-time, but then you never need lead inspections again. Over time, that saves money and hassle. Want me to get you information?"

## Key Takeaways

1. **Fire inspection is state law** - All 1-4 family rentals
2. **Lead inspection for pre-1978** - Every turnover plus every 2 years
3. **Visual vs dust wipe** - Determined by municipality
4. **Demographics don't determine** - It's about building age
5. **Requirements change** - Always verify current rules
6. **Lead-free certification** - One-time permanent solution
7. **Multiple dwellings different** - 3+ units have additional rules
8. **Know your municipalities** - Rules vary by town
