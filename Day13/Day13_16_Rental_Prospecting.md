# Day 13, Topic 16: Rental Property Prospecting

## Overview

Building a rental business provides steady income and creates pathways to larger transactions. This topic covers how to find rental leads, why landlords are the key to success, and strategies for building sustainable rental business.

---

## Why Focus on Rentals

### Building Your Business

**When Starting Out:**
- Rentals often come first
- Lower barrier to entry
- Regular transaction practice
- Builds experience and confidence

**Example:**
> "When she started on Op City, she got nothing but rentals. Nothing but rentals."

### The Transition

**Renters Become Buyers:**
- Build relationships now
- Stay in touch
- They'll call when ready to buy

**Success Story:**
> "She kept in touch with her rentals. Somebody rented a house in Cresskill. She called them on their anniversary. 'I'm so glad you called!' They just bought a $3 million house in Alpine. They wanted to live there first to see if they liked the area."

## Finding Landlord Leads

### MLS Mining

**Process:**
1. Search for recently leased properties
2. Look up addresses
3. Find owner in tax records
4. Contact owners directly
5. Offer to be their rental agent

**Value Proposition:**
"I noticed your property recently leased. I specialize in helping landlords manage their rental process. Could we discuss how I might help with your next vacancy?"

### Online Sources

**Platforms:**
- Craigslist (use caution - many scams)
- For Rent By Owner .com
- Hot Pads
- Zillow Rentals
- Facebook Marketplace

**Craigslist Warning:**
> "Be careful because there's a lot of scams on Craigslist. You can't trust everything."

### Traditional Sources

**Newspapers:**
- Classified sections (diminishing)
- The Record (Bergen County) - less robust now
- Local papers

**Current Reality:**
> "The Record had a pretty good classified section for many years. Now it's really diminished. Not that great anymore."

### Referrals

**Past Tenants:**
- Stay in touch
- Ask for referrals
- They know other renters

**Even Non-Qualifiers:**
> "Even the people that don't qualify, you want to stay in touch with them."

## Why Landlords Are Key

### Repeat Business Reality

**Tenant Turnover:**
- Few people rent for 20 years
- 3-5 year average tenancy
- Each turnover = new transaction
- Same landlord, multiple commissions

**Example:**
> "I have some landlords been with me for 12 years. Very few people rent an apartment and stay there for 20 years."

### Growth Potential

**Landlords Expand:**
- Start with one property
- Add more over time
- May need agent for purchases too

**Real Example:**
> "One landlord has some townhouses. He called me up - 'I got another building.' Found him a townhouse in Cliffside Park. He fixed it up, called me: 'OK, I'm ready to put it on the market for rent.'"

### The Math

**One Landlord Over Time:**
- Year 1: First rental lease
- Year 4: Renewal or new tenant
- Year 5: Buys second property (sale!)
- Year 6: Second property rents
- Year 8: First property new tenant
- Etc.

## Value Proposition for Landlords

### What You Offer

**1. Market Analysis:**
> "We're going to comp their properties and analyze the value."

- Pricing guidance
- Market timing advice
- Competition analysis

**2. Legal Updates:**
> "We can explain all the changes in the laws - especially in the last two years."

- Lead paint requirements
- Inspection requirements
- Fair housing updates
- Lease requirements

**3. Advertising:**
> "Landlord doesn't have to worry about advertising."

- MLS listing
- Online syndication
- Professional marketing
- Wider reach

**4. Tenant Screening:**
> "We're going to have more qualified tenants because we're running credit reports and background checks."

- Credit checks
- Background checks
- Income verification
- Reference checks

**5. Showing Management:**
> "We will schedule the showings. The landlord doesn't have to be there."

- Coordinate times
- Handle access
- Professional presentation
- Time savings for landlord

**6. Interviews:**
> "We will offer an interview."

- Screen potential tenants
- Assess fit
- Present qualified options

**7. Fee Structure (North Jersey):**
> "In North Jersey, typically the tenant pays the fee most of the time."

## Tenant Interviews

### The Debate

**Some Attorneys Disagree:**
> "Some attorneys feel we shouldn't because it could be fair housing violations. I totally disagree."

**Instructor's Position:**
> "I've found that interviews have helped. People should have been turned down because they had a pet, but after the interview, the landlord liked them and the pet."

### Interview Benefits

**For Landlord:**
- See potential tenants
- Assess personality fit
- Ask questions directly
- Build relationship

**For Agent:**
- Gauge tenant quality
- Identify concerns
- Facilitate connection
- Reduce future problems

### Best Practice

**When Landlord Attends:**
> "I love when they talk to each other. I love when I show a unit and the landlord's there. They see my people, get a feeling for them, answer questions on the spot. They're already building a relationship."

---

## Buyer/Seller vs Tenant/Landlord Dynamics

### Different Strategies

**Buyers and Sellers:**
> "I want to keep them away from each other because they can compromise their negotiating position."

**Tenants and Landlords:**
> "Tenants and landlords - I love when they talk to each other."

### Why the Difference

**Purchase Transactions:**
- One-time negotiation
- Adversarial positions possible
- Large stakes
- Emotional decisions

**Rental Relationships:**
- Ongoing relationship
- Need to work together
- Regular interactions
- Mutual interest in good fit

## Staying Connected with Tenants

### Anniversary Calls

**Strategy:**
Call tenants on move-in anniversary

**Script:**
"Hi [Name], it's [Agent] from Christie's. I'm just checking in - it's been a year since you moved in. How's everything going? Anything I can help with?"

### Qualifying Information Changes

**What to Update:**
- Income changes
- Family size changes
- Location preferences
- Timeline for buying

### Future Transactions

**Renters Become:**
- Repeat renters (new location)
- Buyers (when ready)
- Referral sources (friends need housing)
- Landlords (investors starting out)

---

## Managing Part-Time Real Estate

### Working While Building

**The Challenge:**
Many agents start part-time while employed elsewhere.

**Strategies:**
> "If I was working, I'd make my calls either after work or during lunch hour. I'd adjust my lunch to 12-1 or 1-2 to accommodate showings."

### Focus on Quality

**Key Principle:**
> "I didn't try to grab everything. I tried, when I got a client, to work them to close."

**Benefits:**
- Deeper relationships
- Better service
- More referrals
- Sustainable growth

### Transition Planning

**Building Up:**
> "Slowly build up the business to the point where you're ready to make the switchover."

**Time Management:**
> "It helped me be more organized. I had to be."

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: MLS Prospecting
You find 10 recently leased properties in your target area.

**Action Plan:**
1. Look up each address in tax records
2. Identify property owners
3. Research their contact information
4. Draft outreach script
5. Make contact calls
6. Track responses

### Scenario 2: Past Rental Client Check-In
It's been 18 months since you helped someone rent.

**Call Script:**
"Hi [Name], this is [Agent]. I placed you in your apartment about 18 months ago and wanted to check in. How's everything going? Are you still happy there? Any thoughts about your housing plans for the future?"

### Scenario 3: Craigslist Landlord
You see a for-rent-by-owner ad on Craigslist.

**Approach:**
1. Verify legitimacy (not a scam)
2. Research the property
3. Contact with value proposition
4. Offer to help with current listing or future needs
5. Build relationship regardless of immediate business

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Landlords are the real target** - They provide repeat business
2. **Rentals are a starting point** - Build experience and relationships
3. **Stay connected with tenants** - Renters become buyers
4. **Provide value to landlords** - Marketing, screening, legal updates
5. **Let tenants and landlords meet** - Unlike buyers/sellers
6. **MLS is a lead source** - Mine recently leased properties
7. **Anniversary calls work** - Simple touch point with big returns
