# Day 13, Topic 15: Contract & Offer Process

## Overview

The contract and offer process differs significantly between condos and co-ops. This topic covers how to write offers, navigate attorney review, and manage transactions through to successful closing for both property types.

---

## Condo Offer Process

### Standard Process

**Step 1: Write Purchase Contract**
- Use standard NJ real estate contract
- Include all contingencies
- Attach pre-approval letter

**Step 2: Submit to Seller**
- Present offer through listing agent
- Include supporting documents
- Set response deadline

**Step 3: Attorney Review (3 Days)**
- Either attorney can cancel or modify
- Negotiate terms and language
- Ends with approval or termination

**Step 4: Post-Approval**
- Inspections proceed
- Mortgage process continues
- Timeline to closing established

---

## Co-op Offer Process

### Different Documents Required

**Key Difference:**
Co-ops use Offer to Purchase / Letter of Intent (LOI) first.

**Finding in ZIP Forms:**
> "If you're looking for Letter of Intent in ZIP forms, it's not called that - it's 'Offer to Purchase.' I looked for days."

### Step-by-Step Process

**Step 1: Write Offer to Purchase (LOI)**
- Similar to business transactions
- Not legally binding
- Outlines proposed terms

**Step 2: Submit and Negotiate**
- Present to seller
- Negotiate key terms
- Reach agreement in principle

**Step 3: Forward to Attorneys**
> "Upon acceptance, forward the offer to the attorney."

**Step 4: Attorneys Draft Contract**
- Attorneys create binding contract
- Negotiate specific language
- Both parties sign

**Step 5: Schedule Board Meeting**
> "Co-op board will not meet with anybody unless they have an agreed-upon contract. It's not an LOI - it's the contract that the attorney draws up that both sides sign."

---

## Attorney Document Requests

### For Both Condos and Co-ops

**Standard Requests:**
1. **CCRs** - Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions
2. **HOA Financial Statements** - Current financial health
3. **Insurance Policies** - Including flood if applicable
4. **Board Meeting Minutes** - At least 2-3 months

### Why Board Minutes Matter

**What They Reveal:**
- Upcoming assessments
- Pending lawsuits
- Building issues
- Management problems
- Owner disputes

**Example:**
> "We had a security guard altercation. All of that was in the minutes - discussions about suing the homeowner, potential lawsuits, investigations. Buyers need to know."

### Getting Minutes

**Challenge:**
> "Minutes are always hard to get. I don't know why. Usually you can get other things, but minutes sometimes they give you one month."

**Strategy:**
> "Ask for 3-6 months. You're lucky if you get 1-2. In any negotiation, ask for more than what you're looking for."

---

## Working with Attorneys

### Educating Through Questions

**When Attorney Is Inexperienced:**
Rather than telling them what to do, ask questions:
- "When do you think you'll be getting the CCRs?"
- "Have you requested the HOA financials?"
- "Did you request the board minutes?"

**Why This Works:**
> "You just educated them without being disrespectful. Believe me, you'll be surprised how many attorneys don't know - law school was a long time ago."

### Attorney Selection Matters

**Requirements:**
- Licensed in property state
- Experience with property type
- Condo experience for condos
- Co-op experience for co-ops

**Warning:**
> "If your client's attorney has never done a co-op sale - it's different. You need somebody experienced."

## The Inspection Phase

### Standard Inspections

**Home Inspector:**
- Unit-specific inspection
- Systems check
- Surface-level review

**Professional Engineer (When Appropriate):**
- Building-wide assessment
- Pulls permits from City Hall
- Structural analysis
- Better for large buildings

**Real Example:**
> "In Hoboken, I used an engineer. The building was 8 years old with a design flaw - terraces pitched toward building. The engineer found rotting supports. Neither listing agent nor seller disclosed it."

### Inspection Findings

**Unit vs Building Issues:**
- Unit issues = seller responsibility
- Building issues = HOA responsibility
- Distinguish in negotiations
- Different negotiating strategies

## The Mortgage Process

### Standard Milestones

1. **Application** - Full application submitted
2. **Condo Questionnaire** - Lender reviews building
3. **Appraisal** - Property value confirmed
4. **Underwriting** - Full file review
5. **Clear to Close** - All conditions satisfied
6. **Closing Disclosure** - Final numbers confirmed

### Non-Warrantable Challenges

**If Building Isn't Approved:**
- Standard lenders may decline
- Need portfolio lender (Unity Bank)
- Higher down payment required
- Higher interest rate

**Research First:**
> "Ask what banks have already financed in the building. That tells you those banks will lend there."

---

## Co-op Board Phase

### After Contract Signed

**Step 1: Submit Financial Package**
- Organized binder with tabs
- All required documentation
- References included

**Step 2: Board Reviews**
- May take several weeks
- May request additional information

**Step 3: Board Meeting Scheduled**
- Only after signed contract exists
- Buyer attends in person
- Professional presentation

**Step 4: Decision Made**
- Approval allows closing to proceed
- Rejection ends transaction

### Timeline Impact

**Co-ops Take Longer:**
- Add 4-6 weeks for board process
- Set expectations with all parties
- Factor into any time-sensitive situations

---

## Final Walkthrough

### Purpose

**Verify:**
- Condition matches expectations
- Repairs completed (if agreed)
- Nothing removed that shouldn't be
- Nothing added that shouldn't be

### Common Issues

- Appliances removed
- Fixtures changed
- Damage since last visit
- Items left behind

### Resolution

- Note issues before closing
- May negotiate credits
- May delay closing
- Document everything

---

## The Closing

### What Happens

**For Condos:**
- Deed transfers
- Funds exchange
- Keys provided
- Recording occurs

**For Co-ops:**
- Stock transfers
- Proprietary lease assigned
- Board approval confirmed
- No deed (stock certificate instead)

### Post-Closing Actions

**Testimonial Request:**
> "That's the perfect time - at closing when everybody's happy - to ask for a testimonial."

**If Client Too Busy:**
> "Ask ChatGPT to write one. Read it to them: 'Is that OK? Can I use your initials?' Yes."

**Photography:**
> "If you're going to take pictures with your client in front of the building or on their terrace, get permission and send email confirmation."

## Ongoing Relationship

### Stay Connected

**Why It Matters:**
> "The buyers today are the sellers tomorrow."

**Statistics:**
> "National Association of Realtors surveyed sellers - 87-95% would use the agent again. But only 12% actually do. Why? They lost contact."

**The Lesson:**
> "They couldn't even remember the name of the agent."

### Contact Methods

- Anniversary of purchase
- Market updates
- Holiday greetings
- Helpful information
- Referral requests

**Long-Term Results:**
> "Last two months I've had people from 30 years ago call me. They want to sell. If I hadn't kept in touch, they would have never remembered me."

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: First Condo Offer
Writing first purchase contract for condo buyer.

**Process:**
1. Have template contract ready
2. Include standard contingencies (inspection, mortgage, appraisal)
3. Attach pre-approval letter
4. Submit through listing agent
5. Track attorney review timeline

### Scenario 2: Co-op Offer Rejection
Co-op board rejects your buyer.

**Response:**
1. Request feedback (if available)
2. Support disappointed client
3. Discuss alternatives
4. Learn for future
5. Maintain relationship for future transactions

### Scenario 3: Missing Board Minutes
Attorney can't get board minutes from management company.

**Escalation:**
1. Agent contacts management directly
2. Explain buyer's concern
3. Offer to sign for one month minimum
4. Note issue in any negotiations
5. Buyer decides whether to proceed

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Condo = standard contract; Co-op = LOI then contract** - Different processes
2. **Board meeting requires signed contract** - Not just LOI for co-ops
3. **Request board minutes** - Reveals hidden issues
4. **Educate attorneys through questions** - Not all have experience
5. **Engineers see more than inspectors** - For large buildings
6. **Co-ops take longer** - Set timeline expectations
7. **Stay connected** - Buyers become sellers
