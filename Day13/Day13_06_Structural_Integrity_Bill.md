# Day 13, Topic 6: Structural Integrity Bill & Corporate Transparency Act

## Overview

Recent legislation has significantly impacted condominium and cooperative associations. This topic covers the Structural Integrity Bill enacted after the Champlain Towers collapse and the Corporate Transparency Act affecting HOA board members.

---

## The Champlain Towers Disaster

### What Happened

**Location:** Surfside, Florida (June 2021)
**Building:** Champlain Towers South - 12-story beachfront condo
**Outcome:** 98 deaths, building collapse

### Root Causes

**1. Deferred Maintenance:**
- Beautiful lobbies and common areas
- Structural elements neglected
- Money spent on appearances, not bones

**2. Structural Design Flaws:**
- Support beams improperly connected
- Beams attached on sides with bolts
- Should have been sitting ON beams

**3. Spalling:**
> "Rust lines on concrete indicating water presence"

**What Spalling Does:**
- Water penetrates porous concrete
- Reaches internal rebar (metal reinforcement)
- Metal oxidizes and expands
- Can expand up to 7x original size
- Pulverizes surrounding concrete
- Weakens structural integrity

**4. Pool Deck Issues:**
- Deck pitched TOWARD building (should pitch away)
- Drain installed but not maintained
- Water sat against building
- Heavy planters added without structural support
- Pool deck attached to building AND parking garage below

**5. Added Weight:**
- 40-50 year old building
- Original design didn't account for:
  - Marble countertops
  - Quartz and granite
  - Tile floors
  - Modern AC systems on roof

### Special Assessment History

**First Attempt:**
- Board proposed special assessment for repairs
- Owners voted it down
- Repairs not made

**Second Attempt:**
- Years later, new assessment passed
- By then, damage was too severe
- Building collapsed during repair planning

## Structural Integrity Bill

### Purpose

**Response to Champlain:**
- Prevent similar disasters
- Ensure proper maintenance
- Protect condo owners
- Create accountability

### Key Requirements

**1. Mandatory Physical Inspections:**
- Required for certain building types
- Scheduled timeline for compliance
- Professional engineer assessments

**2. Building Types Covered:**
- "Primary load bearing systems"
- All units in one building
- Low-rise, mid-rise, high-rise structures
- Self-contained multi-unit buildings

**What's NOT Covered:**
- Townhouse-style condos
- Row houses that are condos
- Buildings where units aren't all in one structure

### Capital Reserve Fund Requirements

**New Standards:**
- Associations must maintain adequate reserves
- Specific funding levels required
- Plans for future expenditures
- Regular reserve studies

**Impact on Associations:**
> "What's happening with condo associations now - many have had special assessments to add more money to their reserve fund."

**Timeline Concern:**
> "The state expected them to do it over a year instead of giving them a couple of years to build up. This caused many people to put their place on the market because they couldn't afford the assessment."

## Special Assessments

### What They Are

**Definition:**
Additional fee charged to owners to pay for:
- Major repairs
- Reserve fund shortfalls
- Capital improvements
- Emergency expenses

### How They Work

**Typical Process:**
1. Need identified (repair, reserve shortfall)
2. Engineer assessment obtained
3. Multiple contractor bids collected
4. Board proposes special assessment
5. Owners vote on assessment
6. If passed, payment terms set
7. Work completed

### Cost Distribution

**Based On:**
- Unit square footage
- Percentage of common area ownership
- One-bedrooms pay less than three-bedrooms
- Larger units = larger share

**Example:**
> "Our building needed sealing - 176 units, two towers. It was a $5-6 million project. My one-bedroom paid an additional $15,000."

### Payment Options

**Board Decisions:**
- Lump sum payment
- Spread over time (monthly payments)
- Payment plan (2-3 years)

**Good Board Approach:**
> "Our board didn't want people to start selling, so they spread it over a three-year period."

## Reserve Fund Management

### Well-Managed Associations

**Example from Training:**
- Replaced roofs on both towers ($250,000 each)
- Replaced all elevator doors ($200,000)
- Maintained healthy reserves
- No crisis-driven assessments

**Frustration:**
> "I feel like we're getting punished because we did the right thing. We've always run things very well, always had a great reserve account."

### Poorly-Managed Associations

**Warning Signs:**
- Deferred maintenance
- Low reserve balances
- No reserve study
- History of emergency assessments
- Visible deterioration

### What Buyers Should Ask

1. What's in the reserve fund?
2. When was last reserve study?
3. Any special assessments discussed?
4. What major expenses are anticipated?
5. Review last 2-3 years of board minutes

## Corporate Transparency Act

### Purpose

**Original Intent:**
- Prevent money laundering
- Combat tax fraud
- Track corporate ownership
- Anti-terrorism financing measures

### Requirements

**What's Required:**
- Disclosure of board member information
- Personal information including SSN
- Beneficial ownership reporting

### Controversy

**HOA/Co-op Concerns:**
- Board members are volunteers
- They don't have access to association funds
- Only officers can write checks
- Privacy concerns significant

**Response:**
> "A lot of the boards got together and sued the federal government on this one - that's still in the courts."

### Current Status

**As of Training Date:**
- Still in litigation
- Requirements being challenged
- Outcome uncertain
- Check current status before advising clients

## Similar Local Assessments

### Municipal Special Assessments

**Examples:**
- Sidewalk replacement
- Sewer line installation
- Water line upgrades
- Lead pipe replacement

**Wyckoff Example:**
- Town converting from septic to sewer
- $10,000 per homeowner
- Spread over 10 years ($1,000/year)
- Town floated bonds for upfront costs

**Ridgewood Example:**
- Lead pipe replacement program
- Homeowners being assessed
- Required infrastructure upgrade

### Parallel to Condo Assessments

**Similarities:**
- Owners don't choose timing
- Required by authority
- Payment plans may be offered
- Necessary infrastructure work

**Agent Advice:**
> "Don't be afraid of special assessments in condos - even single-family homeowners get assessed for sidewalks and infrastructure."

## Impact on Buyers and Sellers

### For Buyers

**Due Diligence:**
1. Request reserve fund statement
2. Ask about planned assessments
3. Review board meeting minutes
4. Understand building age and condition
5. Factor potential assessments into budget

**Questions for Management:**
- "Is the building compliant with new structural requirements?"
- "When was the last engineering inspection?"
- "Are any special assessments being discussed?"

### For Sellers

**Disclosure Obligations:**
- Known assessment discussions
- Reserve fund status
- Building inspection results
- Upcoming capital projects

**Timing Considerations:**
- Selling before assessment passed = buyer's concern
- Selling after assessment = may need to pay first
- Assessment pending = disclose and price accordingly

### For Agents

**Education Role:**
- Explain new requirements to clients
- Help them understand why assessments may occur
- Set realistic expectations
- Include in property evaluations

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Buyer Concerned About Assessment
"I heard there might be a special assessment. Should I still buy?"

**Response:**
"Let's get the facts first. I'll request the board minutes and reserve study. We need to know: Is an assessment actually being discussed? How much? What's it for? Is it for necessary maintenance that will protect your investment? Sometimes assessments are good - they mean the building is being properly maintained."

### Scenario 2: Seller Facing Assessment
"A $20,000 assessment was just announced. Should I sell before it's due?"

**Response:**
"You'll need to disclose the pending assessment regardless. Buyers will factor it into their offers. You can either pay the assessment and sell without it affecting negotiations, or price your unit to account for the buyer paying it. Let's discuss what makes more financial sense for your situation."

### Scenario 3: Old Building, No Recent Inspection
Buyer interested in 45-year-old high-rise with no recent structural inspection.

**Response:**
"Given the new Structural Integrity Bill requirements, this building will need inspection. If it hasn't been done, it could mean upcoming assessments for compliance. I'd recommend requesting information about their compliance timeline and any engineering reports before proceeding."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Champlain Towers changed everything** - New laws are direct response
2. **Reserve funds now mandated** - Higher levels required
3. **Special assessments increasing** - Many associations playing catch-up
4. **Corporate Transparency Act contested** - Check current status
5. **Due diligence essential** - Review minutes and reserves
6. **Assessments aren't always bad** - They mean proper maintenance
7. **Disclosure is mandatory** - Both known issues and pending assessments
