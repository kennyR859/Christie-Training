# Day 13, Topic 14: Showing Condos Best Practices

## Overview

Showing condos and co-ops requires additional preparation compared to single-family homes. This topic covers pre-showing reconnaissance, day-of-showing best practices, and professional techniques for maintaining confidence throughout the process.

---

## Pre-Visit Reconnaissance

### Why Visit in Advance

**Benefits:**
- Know where to park
- Locate amenities
- Understand access procedures
- Build relationship with staff
- Prevent embarrassing confusion

**The Alternative:**
> "The worst thing is to be looking around for where the unit is while you're showing. You don't want to be looking for things while you're with clients."

### What to Discover

**Essential Information:**
1. Parking for you and clients
2. Building access procedures
3. Unit location
4. Amenity locations
5. Special access requirements

### Building Staff Introduction

**Approach:**
> "Hi, my business card - I'm going to be showing a unit here tomorrow. Where should I park? Where should my clients park?"

**Why This Matters:**
- Staff can provide insider tips
- Builds relationship for future
- Gets accurate information
- Shows professionalism

---

## Locating Amenities

### Common Amenities to Find

- Pool
- Fitness center
- Business center
- Theater room
- Party room
- Storage areas
- Garage/parking
- Mail room
- Concierge desk

### Getting Directions

**Ask Building Staff:**
> "Ask whoever that representative is - where is the storage area, business center, theater room, whatever amenities are in there."

**Then Physically Go:**
- Take directions and walk it
- Verify locations
- Note any access requirements
- Time how long it takes

### Special Access Needs

**Some Amenities Require:**
- Key card
- Special pass
- Reservation
- Staff escort

**Find Out In Advance:**
> "Sometimes they need a special pass or key to get in - even if you know where it is, you need to know how to access it."

## Unit Access Logistics

### Lock Box Identification

**The Challenge:**
> "There were 15 lock boxes on the bicycle rack. I called the listing agent - 'it's the 3rd one from the right.' It was so crazy."

**Solutions:**
- Put colored ribbon on your listing's lock box
- Use distinctive marker
- Cannot put unit number on box
- Make it identifiable

### Access Methods

| Method | Preparation Needed |
|--------|-------------------|
| Lock box on door | Just need code |
| Lock box on rack | Identify which one |
| Concierge/doorman | Allow extra time |
| Agent office pickup | Factor travel time |
| Owner present | Coordinate schedule |

### Time Management

**Allow Buffer For:**
- Key pickup delays
- Concierge on break
- Parking challenges
- Construction blocking entrance

> "All kinds of things can happen. Make sure you allow plenty of time to get that key and meet your client on time."

## Day of Showing

### Arrival Strategy

**Golden Rule:**
Arrive BEFORE your clients.

**Why:**
> "The thing to do is NOT get there after your clients."

**What Can Go Wrong:**
- Entrance blocked for construction
- GPS inaccurate
- Parking unavailable
- Key issue arises
- Flat tire en route

**Tom Coughlin Rule:**
> "Tom Coughlin's 8:00 meeting - if you weren't there by 7:48, you were late."

### Professional Appearance

**Footwear Consideration:**
- Easy on/off shoes (slip-ons)
- May need to remove at unit door
- May need booties
- Walking through common areas

**Be Prepared:**
- Bring booties if not provided
- Wear professional but practical attire
- Be ready to walk to amenities

## Show Best Unit First

### The Strategy

**Always Show Best First:**
> "Show the best unit first. That's the one they measure others against. It creates urgency."

### Why This Matters

**Scenario:**
Showing 4 units, best one is last:
- Client spends extra time at earlier units
- Running late
- "I have to pick up the kids"
- Best unit never seen

**Result:**
> "If you show the best one first, that's most important. If they have to leave early, at least they saw your best option."

### Applying to Houses Too

> "Even when you're showing one to four family houses, show the best one first."

## Handling Unknown Questions

### The Professional Response

**Framework:**
> "[Name], that's a great question. I'm going to get back to you on that. Now, let's take a look at the living room."

**What This Accomplishes:**
1. Uses their name (people like hearing their name)
2. Gives compliment (great question)
3. Provides assurance (will follow up)
4. Pivots away (keeps showing moving)
5. Maintains confidence

### Multiple Unknown Questions

**If It Happens Again:**
> "Fern, you're just full of great questions! I'm going to get back to you on that one too. But let's go look at the backyard."

### The Key Element

**Confidence:**
> "The way I say it is with confidence. She hasn't lost confidence in me, and she knows I'll get back to her."

---

## Confidence and Trust

### Why Confidence Matters

> "People need to learn to trust you. If they think you don't know what you're doing and they don't know what they're doing, they're going to walk to somebody else."

### Building Confidence

**Through Preparation:**
- Visit building in advance
- Know where things are
- Have answers ready
- Look professional

**Through Communication:**
- Never show deer in headlights
- Don't hesitate when asked questions
- Have a system for unknowns
- Follow through on promised answers

### The Worst Scenario

> "The worst thing in the world is to walk with your clients and have THEM find something. It lowers you - you lose confidence."

## Working with Tenants

### Rental Units

**When Showing Occupied Rentals:**
- Meet tenant before showing day
- Build relationship with tenant
- Understand their schedule
- Accommodate reasonable requests

**Tenant Cooperation:**
> "You want to be their friend. You don't want to be adversarial."

### Tenant Warnings

**Risk:**
> "Be there FIRST. If the customer gets there first and talks to the tenant, the tenant might say 'this is a horrible house, the basement leaks.' Tenants don't want to leave."

### Incentivizing Cooperation

**When Tenants Resist:**
> "Talk to the landlord. Maybe if they had a little skin in the game - take $10-15 off rent every time they allow a showing, save business cards, confirm with me - they might be more cooperative."

## Using Technology

### ChatGPT for Preparation

**Applications:**
> "Go to ChatGPT and say 'what is the list of questions potential buyers might ask about a condo and how would I answer it?'"

**Benefits:**
- Practice common questions
- Prepare objection handling
- Learn dialog patterns
- Build confidence

### Phone Tools

**Essential Apps:**
- Compass (for Feng Shui/direction)

- Camera (documentation)

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Can't Find Amenity
Client wants to see gym. You don't know where it is.

**Response:**
"The gym is one of the great amenities here. Let me check with the concierge for the quickest route."

Then make mental note to visit in advance next time.

### Scenario 2: Question You Can't Answer
"What's the exact square footage of the storage unit?"

**Response:**
"That's a great question, and I want to give you the exact number. Let me get that confirmed and I'll have it for you today. Now, let's take a look at how the light comes through these windows."

### Scenario 3: Client Arrives First
You're running late, client texts they're waiting.

**Response:**
Call immediately. Apologize briefly. Give ETA. Ask them to wait in lobby/car rather than exploring alone. Arrive as quickly and professionally as possible.

## Questions to Ask Listing Agent

### Before Every Showing

1. What's included in maintenance?
2. Any special assessments being discussed?
3. Management company or self-managed?
4. Any lawsuits against HOA?
5. Parking deeded or assigned?
6. Any showing restrictions?

### Access Questions

1. Lock box location?
2. Concierge procedure?
3. Parking for showing?
4. Shoe removal required?
5. Any pets to be aware of?

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Visit building in advance** - Know where everything is
2. **Arrive before clients** - Always
3. **Show best unit first** - They may leave early
4. **Have system for unknowns** - Maintains confidence
5. **Build tenant relationships** - They can help or hurt
6. **Use technology** - ChatGPT for prep, apps for tools
7. **Preparation = confidence** - Clients trust prepared agents
