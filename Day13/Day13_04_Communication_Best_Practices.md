# Day 13, Topic 4: Text and Email Communication Best Practices

## Overview

Effective communication in real estate requires understanding how messages can be misinterpreted and how to protect yourself through proper documentation. This topic covers the Zig Ziglar principle of communication, best practices for text and email, and strategies for protecting yourself and your clients.

---

## The Zig Ziglar Principle

### Who Was Zig Ziglar

**Background:**
- National sales trainer
- Started selling pots door-to-door
- Became number one salesman by cooking dinners for prospects
- Taught communication principles used across industries

### The Seven Meanings Principle

**Core Concept:**
> "You can say the same sentence seven times and mean seven different things."

**Example: "I said he beat his wife"**
| Emphasis | Interpretation |
|----------|----------------|
| **I** said he beat his wife | It was me who said it |
| I **said** he beat his wife | I definitely communicated it |
| I said **he** beat his wife | He specifically, not someone else |
| I said he **beat** his wife | The action of beating |
| I said he beat **his** wife | His wife, not someone else's |
| I said he beat his **wife** | Wife, not another person |
| Normal reading | Statement of fact |

### Application to Real Estate

**The Problem:**
- We know what we're trying to say
- We don't know how they're receiving it
- Text lacks tone, facial expressions, context
- Misunderstandings damage relationships

## Text Message Guidelines

### Appropriate Uses

**Good for:**
- Appointment confirmations
- Meeting times and locations
- Quick status updates
- "Call me" requests
- Simple yes/no questions

**Examples of Good Texts:**
```
"Confirmed for showing at 123 Main St, 2pm tomorrow"
"Can you talk? Have quick question about the offer"
"FYI - inspection scheduled for Tuesday at 10am"
```

### Inappropriate Uses

**Avoid for:**
- Complex negotiations
- Emotional discussions
- Contract terms
- Legal matters
- Disputes

**Why:**
> "On a text, we know how we're texting and what we're trying to say, but we don't know how they're receiving it."

### Response Expectations

**Reality:**
- Texts create expectation of immediate response
- Initial reactions may not be well-considered
- People respond emotionally under pressure
- Better for time-sensitive coordination

## Email Best Practices

### Advantages of Email

1. **More Space:** Room to explain fully
2. **Time to Compose:** Can think before sending
3. **Time-Stamped:** Creates documented record
4. **Searchable:** Can find later
5. **Attachments:** Send documents easily

### When to Use Email

**Appropriate For:**
- Documenting agreements
- Confirming verbal conversations
- Sending detailed information
- Creating paper trail
- Following up important matters

**Example Uses:**
```
- Confirmation of advice given
- Summary of meeting discussion
- Property information packets
- Inspection reports
- Contract clarifications
```

### Email Management Challenges

**Reality Check:**
> "I'm getting so many spam emails. I must erase maybe 100 spam emails a day."

**Best Practices:**
- Create filters for important contacts
- Use subject lines clearly
- Follow up important emails with call
- Don't assume email was read

## Documentation for Protection

### Why Documentation Matters

**Scenario Without Documentation:**
- Client later claims "no one told me"
- Your word against theirs
- No proof of advice given
- Potential liability

**Scenario With Documentation:**
- Time-stamped text or email exists
- Manager also documented advice
- Clear record of recommendation
- Protection in disputes

### Critical Documentation Situations

**Always Document:**
1. Attorney recommendation to clients
2. Inspection waiver discussions
3. Appraisal contingency decisions
4. Price reduction conversations
5. Any unusual client decisions

### Documentation Method

**Step by Step:**
1. Give verbal advice
2. Send confirming text or email
3. Call manager to also advise client
4. Save all communications
5. Note in transaction file

**Example - Attorney Advice:**
```
Text sent: "As I mentioned, I strongly recommend
you hire a real estate attorney. Please confirm
you received this advice."

Called Dale (manager) to also advise client.
```

## Text Message Records

### Never Delete

**Why Keep Everything:**
- May need months or years later
- Proves advice given
- Shows timeline of events
- Protects against claims

**Reality:**
> "You have to keep your texts especially if you're telling somebody to do something and you want to make sure you're giving them good advice."

### Storage Challenges

**Common Issues:**
- Phone storage fills up
- Old messages hard to find
- Backup needed for phone loss
- Archiving methods vary

**Solutions:**
- Screenshot important exchanges
- Backup to cloud
- Email yourself summaries
- Use CRM to log communications

## Handling Sensitive Communications

### When to Avoid Writing

**Some Matters Better in Person:**
- Highly emotional situations
- Complex negotiations
- Delivering bad news
- Resolving conflicts

**The Instructor's Approach:**
> "I'm a little different when it comes to putting things in writing on text. I try to limit it to 'meet you at 6:00' - things that are more definite."

### Switching to Phone

**When Text Isn't Working:**
- Multiple back-and-forth needed
- Confusion arising
- Emotions running high
- Complex explanation required

**Rule:**
> "If I don't answer the text, call."

---

## Keeping Parties Separate

### Buyer/Seller Communication

**General Rule:**
- Keep buyers and sellers apart
- You are the spokesperson
- Protects negotiating positions
- Prevents emotional decisions

**Risk of Direct Contact:**
> "They can compromise their negotiating position if they talk to each other."

### Exception: Tenant/Landlord

**Different Dynamic:**
- Let tenants and landlords interact
- Builds relationship for approval
- Answers questions directly
- Helps with decision comfort

---

## Communication with Other Agents

### Professional Courtesy

**Never Say to Clients:**
- "That agent is stubborn"
- "The other side is unreasonable"
- "Their agent doesn't know what they're doing"

**Why:**
> "Never complain about the seller or buyer to the other person. That's the only thing a person might need as an excuse - 'forget it, that person's horrible.'"

### Reframing Perspective

**Better Approach:**
When client frustrated with other party:
> "If you put yourself in their position - if this were your house - what would you be thinking?"

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Client Refuses Attorney
After verbal advice to hire an attorney, client still refuses.

**Documentation:**
```
Text: "[Name], I want to confirm our conversation.
I strongly advise you to hire a real estate attorney
for this transaction. If you choose not to, please
understand you may not have legal representation
during negotiations. Please acknowledge receipt."

Then: Call manager to also make recommendation.
```

### Scenario 2: Complex Negotiation via Text
Buyer wants to negotiate multiple counter-offer points through text.

**Response:**
"These are good points to discuss. Given the complexity, let's schedule a call to work through each item. What time works for you today?"

### Scenario 3: Emotional Client
Client sends angry text about other party's demands.

**Response:**
"I understand your frustration. Let me look into this and call you in 15 minutes to discuss. Sometimes there's more context that helps."

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Same words, different meanings** - Text lacks context
2. **Text for confirmation, email for documentation** - Know when to use each
3. **Never delete messages** - They're your protection
4. **Document critical advice** - Attorney recommendations especially
5. **Multiple documentation methods** - Text + manager + file notes
6. **Keep parties separate** - Protect negotiating positions
7. **Never badmouth other side** - Damages deals and relationships
8. **Technology helps** - CRM and tools for better communication
