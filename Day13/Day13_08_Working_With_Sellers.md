# Day 13, Topic 8: Working with Condo/Co-op Sellers

## Overview

Listing condos and co-ops requires specific preparation and knowledge beyond traditional home sales. This topic covers the essential information agents need to gather, access logistics, and how to prepare sellers for successful transactions.

---

## Listing Preparation Checklist

### Required Documents and Information

**Certificate of Occupancy:**
- Required in some municipalities
- Verify local requirements
- May need to be current

**Fire Prevention Inspection:**
- State law requirement
- Must be completed for rental/sale
- Landlord/seller responsibility

**Lead Paint Inspection (if applicable):**
- Pre-1978 buildings
- Two types: visual and dust wipe
- Municipality determines which required

### Financial Information

**From Management Company:**
- [ ] Current monthly maintenance fee
- [ ] What's included in maintenance
- [ ] Parking fees (if separate)
- [ ] Storage unit fees
- [ ] Recent special assessments
- [ ] Upcoming planned assessments

**From Seller:**
- [ ] Payment history
- [ ] Any outstanding balances
- [ ] Assessment obligations

### Access Information

**Essential Questions:**
1. Building showing hours/restrictions
2. Access method (lockbox, key, concierge)
3. Where to park
4. Guest parking availability
5. Move-in/move-out procedures
6. Photography/video permissions

---

## Access and Showing Logistics

### Types of Building Access

**1. Lockbox on Unit Door:**
- Most convenient for agents
- 24/7 access capability
- Standard showing process

**2. Lockbox on Common Area:**
- Bicycle rack, fence, etc.
- Must clearly identify YOUR lockbox
- Use ribbon or marker
- Don't put unit number on box

**3. Concierge/Doorman:**
- Must check in each time
- May have visitor log
- Can be time-consuming
- Allow extra time for showings

**4. Listing Agent Office:**
- Pickup and return required
- Most inconvenient
- May reduce showings
- Discuss alternatives with seller

**5. Owner Present:**
- Schedule around owner
- Less flexibility
- May inhibit buyers

### Buildings Without Lockboxes

**Examples:**
- River Renaissance (East Rutherford) - no lockboxes
- Some Fort Lee high-rises - concierge only
- Various luxury buildings - security protocols

**Agent Advocacy:**
> "I've actually sent letters to some complexes saying 'your HOA is working against you.' If agents have to pick up and return keys, they won't show your property."

**Impact on Value:**
> "The less your place is shown, the less value you have in your place."

## Seller Instructions

### Time and Access Restrictions

**Document Everything:**
- Best times to show
- Times to avoid
- Weekend vs weekday preferences
- Holiday restrictions

**Common Restrictions:**
- No showings before 9 AM
- No showings after 8 PM
- 24-hour notice required
- No Sunday showings

### Property Condition Requirements

**Typical Requests:**
- Remove shoes or wear booties
- Don't open certain areas
- Pets to be secured/removed
- Specific lighting preferences
- Temperature settings

**Documentation:**
Put all restrictions in MLS notes and communicate to showing agents.

### Pet Considerations

**Important Information:**
- Pet type and location during showings
- How to handle (crate, neighbor, remove)
- Any pet-free showing requirements
- Allergies of potential buyers

## Working with Building Staff

### Building Relationships

**Concierge/Doorman:**
- Introduce yourself professionally
- Provide business card
- Ask about building procedures
- Build rapport for future transactions

**What to Ask:**
> "Hi, I'm showing a unit here tomorrow. Where should I park? Where should my clients park?"

### Getting Information

**From Building Staff:**
- Parking procedures
- Amenity locations
- Access protocols
- Building quirks
- Showing tips

**Why This Matters:**
> "If there's a concierge, there's usually somebody you can talk to. They can tell you things about the building you need to know."

## Seller Communication

### Setting Expectations

**Condo-Specific Factors:**
- Market competition within building
- Impact of other units for sale
- HOA rules affecting showing
- Assessment impact on pricing

**Co-op-Specific Factors:**
- Board approval process
- Buyer qualification requirements
- Longer transaction timeline
- Possible buyer rejection

### Pricing Discussion

**Factors to Address:**
- Stack comparables
- Floor level impact
- View premium/discount
- Update level vs competition
- Assessment obligations

### Timeline Expectations

**Condo Timeline:**
- Similar to single-family
- Standard mortgage process
- No board approval (usually)

**Co-op Timeline:**
- Longer due to board process
- Board meeting scheduling
- Financial review period
- Possible rejection risk

## Photography and Marketing

### Getting Permission

**For Marketing Photos:**
- Verbal permission from owner
- Follow up with email confirmation
- Document their agreement
- Time-stamped record

**At Closing:**
> "If you're going to take pictures with your client in front of the building or on their terrace, get permission. Send them an email: 'thanks for approving use of your picture for marketing.'"

### Building-Specific Rules

**Some Buildings Restrict:**
- Hallway photography
- Common area photos
- Exterior signage
- Drone photography

**Always Verify:**
- Check HOA rules
- Ask management company
- Get written permission
- Document compliance

### Marketing Considerations

**Condo Marketing:**
- Emphasize building amenities
- Highlight included parking
- Feature views
- Note proximity to transit

**Co-op Marketing:**
- Target qualified buyers
- Note strong financials required
- Emphasize building stability
- May limit marketing to reduce unqualified inquiries

## Handling Inspections

### Pre-Listing Inspection Option

**Benefits:**
- Identifies issues early
- Allows repair before listing
- Prevents surprises
- Stronger negotiating position

### Access for Inspectors

**Coordinate:**
- Building access requirements
- Time needed for inspection
- Common area access
- Unit access arrangements

### Building-Wide Issues

**What Inspector May Find:**
- Individual unit issues (seller responsibility)
- Building-wide issues (HOA responsibility)
- Distinguish in report
- Affects negotiations differently

### For Closing

**Documents Seller Must Provide:**
- HOA contact information
- Management company details

- Reserve fund information
- CCRs (Covenants, Conditions, Restrictions)
- Board meeting minutes

**Maintain Files:**

## Practice Scenarios

### Scenario 1: No Lockbox Allowed
Building doesn't allow lockboxes and requires concierge access.

**Solution:**
1. Document access procedure clearly
2. Provide detailed MLS instructions
3. Give agents concierge name/number
4. Suggest confirming day before showing
5. Discuss with seller impact on showings
6. Price may need to reflect reduced access

### Scenario 2: Special Assessment Pending
$15,000 assessment announced week before listing.

**Approach:**
1. Disclose immediately in listing
2. Adjust pricing accordingly
3. Discuss options with seller:
   - Pay before closing
   - Credit buyer at closing
   - Price reduction
4. Document in all marketing

### Scenario 3: Co-op with Strict Board
Known for rejecting buyers.

**Strategy:**
1. Research rejection patterns
2. Pre-qualify buyers more strictly
3. Prepare buyers for process
4. Help buyers present well
5. Manage seller expectations
6. Build longer timeline into planning

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Gather comprehensive information** - Fees, rules, access, restrictions
2. **Access impacts value** - Easier access = more showings = better results
3. **Document all permissions** - Photography, marketing, access
4. **Build building relationships** - Staff can be valuable allies
5. **Set realistic expectations** - Especially for co-ops
6. **Know the differences** - Condo vs co-op seller experience differs
7. **Prepare for assessments** - Disclose and price accordingly
